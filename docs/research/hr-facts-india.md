## 1. Labour Codes: what is in force and key employee-facing changes

### 1.0 Status
- **Rule:** All four Labour Codes and their Central Rules are in force. Most states' own rules were still pending in October 2026, so for state-sphere employers (most private businesses) some details still depend on old state rules or draft rules.
- **Numbers and dates:**
  - **Codes in force from 21 Nov 2025:** Code on Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations Code, 2020; Code on Social Security, 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. Together they replace 29 central labour laws.
  - **Commencement notifications** (all dated 21 Nov 2025, per India Code footnotes):
    - Wages: S.O. 5322(E). Its Central Advisory Board bits were already in force from 18 Dec 2020 under S.O. 4604(E).
    - IR: S.O. 5320(E).
    - SS: S.O. 5319(E), covering most sections. It lists certain exceptions, and s.142 has been in force since 3 May 2021.
    - OSH: S.O. 5321(E).
  - **Draft Central Rules:** published 30 Dec 2025 (SS draft G.S.R. 935(E)).
  - **Final Central Rules:** notified 8 May 2026: Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026; Industrial Relations (Central) Rules, 2026; Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 344(E)). The OSH (Central) Rules, 2026 are cited as G.S.R. 345(E) dated 8 May 2026, but one law firm gives the publication date as 9 May 2026.
  - **Model Standing Orders, 2026:** S.O. 2312(E), 8 May 2026 (manufacturing, mining, services).
  - **State rules:** at 1 Oct 2026, final rules had been notified only by a minority of states/UTs. Lists differ by Code; they include Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Bihar, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh, J&K, Ladakh and Lakshadweep. Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal and Kerala were not on those lists. The Centre said it expected all states/UTs to publish rules by 31 Oct 2026. **I could not verify the position after that date.**
- **Transition:**
  - The Labour Ministry's FAQs (30 Dec 2025) say old rules continue until new rules are notified, to the extent they are consistent with the Codes (General Clauses Act, s.6).
  - SS Code s.164(2)(b) keeps schemes and regulations under repealed Acts alive for one year, to 20 Nov 2026. EPF/EPS/EDLI have already been replaced (29 Jun 2026); the ESI replacements are awaited.

### 1.1 "Wages" definition and the 50% rule
- **Rule:** "Wages" means all pay, allowances and other remuneration, minus a fixed list of exclusions. If the excluded items (a) to (i) add up to more than half of total remuneration, the excess is added back into "wages".
- **Numbers and definition:**
  - Included expressly: basic pay, dearness allowance (DA) and retaining allowance.
  - Because the starting point is "all remuneration", an allowance that is not on the exclusion list (for example a generic "special allowance") is part of wages.
  - Exclusions:
    - (a) statutory bonus that is not part of contractual pay
    - (b) value of house accommodation and amenities
    - (c) employer PF/pension contribution
    - (d) conveyance allowance
    - (e) sums to defray special expenses
    - (f) HRA
    - (g) award/settlement payments
    - (h) overtime allowance
    - (i) commission
    - (j) gratuity
    - (k) retrenchment compensation, retirement benefits and ex gratia on termination
  - **50% proviso:** if (a) to (i) together exceed one-half of all remuneration, the excess is deemed wages. The Centre may notify a different percentage; none was found.
  - **In-kind pay:** counts only up to 15% of total wages.
  - **Equal pay and payment of wages:** for these purposes, (d), (f), (g) and (h) are counted in wages.
- **Ministry FAQs (non-binding):**
  - Performance incentives, ESOPs, variable pay and reimbursements are not wages.
  - Overtime counts toward the 50% test.
  - The definition applies from 21 Nov 2025.
  - The SS Code definition governs ESI contributions from that date.
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** Code on Wages s.2(y); the SS Code's identical definition is s.2(88).
- **Sources:**
  - [Code on Wages, 2019 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15793/1/aA2019-29.pdf)
  - [Code on Social Security, 2020 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16823/1/aA2020-36.pdf)
  - [SCC Online: Labour Ministry issued FAQs on the Labour Codes (3 Jan 2026)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/01/03/labour-ministry-issued-faqs-on-the-labour-codes/)

- **Confidence:** High for the statutory text. Medium for how "all remuneration" is measured in the 50% test: it is not settled whether, for example, the employer's PF contribution is counted. The FAQs are non-binding.

### 1.2 Full and final settlement (F&F) timeline
- **Rule:** When an employee resigns, or is removed, dismissed or retrenched, or loses the job because the establishment closes, the employer must pay the wages due within two working days.
- **Numbers:**
  - **Wages:** 2 working days (Code on Wages s.17(2)). This applies to every "employee", including managerial and supervisory staff. Chapter III has no wage ceiling; only government establishments are carved out (s.25).
  - **Encashment of unused annual leave for OSH "workers":** before the second working day expires after discharge, dismissal or quitting. On superannuation or death, within 2 months (OSH Code s.32(1)(vi)).
  - **Gratuity:** separate timeline of 30 days from when it becomes payable (SS Code s.56(3)); see topic 2.
  - **Model Standing Orders (Schedules A/B):** wages and dues on termination are due before the second working day expires.
  - **Normal monthly pay cycle:** wages are due before the 7th of the following month (s.17(1)).
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** Code on Wages s.17(1)–(2); OSH Code s.32(1)(vi); SS Code s.56(3); Model Standing Orders 2026, clauses 13(3) and 23(3).
- **Sources:**
  - [Code on Wages, 2019 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15793/1/aA2019-29.pdf)
  - [OSH Code, 2020 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22041/1/2020-37.pdf)
  - [Model Standing Orders 2026 (taxheal)](https://www.taxheal.com/model-standing-orders-2026.html)
  - [LiveLaw: Two Working Days: Code On Wages And Employee's Final Settlement (3 Oct 2026)](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/two-working-days-code-wages-employee-final-settlement-552780)
- **Confidence:** High for the 2-working-day rule.
- **Caveats:**
  1. The statute says "within two working days of … his resignation". Commentators read the trigger as the date the resignation takes effect (the last working day), not the date the letter is handed in. **No official clarification was found.**
  2. Whether leave encashment counts as "wages" for s.17(2) is debated. For OSH "workers" it has its own 2-working-day rule anyway.
  3. Superannuation and retirement are not expressly listed in s.17(2).

### 1.3 Annual leave with wages (OSH Code)
- **Rule:** A "worker" who works at least 180 days in a calendar year earns paid annual leave at 1 day for every 20 days worked. Up to 30 days can be carried forward, and leave can be encashed.
- **Numbers:**
  - **Eligibility:** 180 days or more worked in the calendar year. Lay-off days, maternity leave and leave taken count toward the 180 days, but no leave accrues on them.
  - **Accrual:** 1 day per 20 days worked. Adolescents and workers below ground in mines earn 1 day per 15.
  - **Mid-year joiners:** a reduced eligibility test applies (s.32(1)(v)).
  - **Carry-forward:** maximum 30 days. Leave applied for and refused can be carried forward without limit.
  - **Encashment:** on the worker's demand at the end of the calendar year. Leave above 30 days is encashable.
  - **On separation:** payment as in 1.2.
- **Who is covered:** "workers". This excludes people employed mainly in a managerial or administrative capacity, and supervisors drawing wages above ₹18,000 a month.
- **Applies in:** OSH "establishments", i.e. 10 or more workers (OSH s.2(1)(v)).
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** OSH Code s.32; record in Form XX under OSH Central Rule 76.
- **Sources:**
  - [OSH Code, 2020 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22041/1/2020-37.pdf)
  - [TaxClue: Section 32 OSH Code 2020, annual leave with wages](https://www.taxclue.in/blog/section-32-osh-code-2020-annual-leave-with-wages/)
  - [SCC Online: Ministry FAQs (26 Apr 2026)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/26/ministry-faqs-four-labour-codes-india-2026/)
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Caveat:** state Shops & Establishments (S&E) Acts continue alongside the Code, and some are more generous (Karnataka and Maharashtra allow 45 days carry-forward; see topic 8). Employers usually apply whichever rule is more beneficial. How the Code and the state Acts interact is not settled.

### 1.4 Working hours and overtime
See topic 10. Headline: 8 hours a day, 48 hours a week, no more than 6 days a week, and overtime at twice the ordinary rate with the worker's consent.

### 1.5 Fixed-term employees (FTE)
- **Rule:** A fixed-term worker must get at least the same hours, wages, allowances and benefits as a comparable permanent worker. Statutory benefits accrue proportionately, and gratuity is payable after one year of service under the contract.
- **Numbers:**
  - Gratuity eligibility after 1 year. An 11-month contract that ends on expiry does not qualify (Ministry FAQ).
  - Non-renewal or expiry of the contract is not retrenchment, and no notice is due under the Model Standing Orders (clause 23(2)).
  - Early non-disciplinary termination may count as retrenchment.
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** IR Code s.2(o), provisos (a) to (c); SS Code s.53; Social Security (Central) Rules 2026, Rule 33(1).
- **Sources:**
  - [IR Code, 2020 (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22040/1/aa202035.pdf)
  - [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan: Top 10 FAQs on Fixed Term Employment (updated 26 Mar 2026)](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/top-10-faqs-on-fixed-term-employment-fte)
  - [PIB (21 Nov 2025)](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/pib2192463.pdf)
- **Confidence:** High for parity and the one-year gratuity rule. Medium for how an early exit is treated.

### 1.6 Appointment letters
- **Rule:** Every employee must receive an appointment letter on joining. Existing employees without one had to be given one within three months of the OSH Code's commencement, i.e. by about 20 Feb 2026.
- **Contents:** the format is prescribed in the OSH Central Rules 2026. Secondary sources list name, date of birth, Aadhaar, designation/nature of employment, date of joining, establishment labour ID, social-security applicability and wage components.
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** OSH Code s.6(1)(f); OSH (Central) Rules 2026 (format).
- **Sources:**

### 1.7 Other employee-facing points worth one line each (all from PIB, 21 Nov 2025; High)
- Minimum wages now cover all employees, and a national floor wage applies. Floor-wage amounts had not been notified as of May 2026 (BDO).
- Women may work night shifts with consent and safeguards.
- Employers must provide a free annual health check-up for workers aged 40 and above.
- ESIC coverage now applies across India (see topic 4).

## 2. Gratuity

- **Rule:** On leaving after at least 5 years' continuous service, an employee gets 15 days' wages for each year of service. Monthly-rated wages are divided by 26, and the total is capped (currently ₹20 lakh).
- **Formula (monthly-rated):** Gratuity = last drawn monthly wages × 15 ÷ 26 × years of service. Exceptions:
  - Seasonal establishments: 7 days' wages per season.
  - Piece-rated employees: daily wage = average of the last 3 months, excluding overtime.
- **Part-years:** each completed year counts, plus one more year if the remaining part is **more than six months**.
- **Eligibility:**
  - **Standard:** 5 years' continuous service, ending in superannuation, retirement, resignation, death or disablement from accident or disease, or expiry of a fixed term.
  - **Continuous service:** a year counts if the employee worked 240 days in the preceding 12 months, or 190 days in mines below ground or in establishments working fewer than 6 days a week (s.54).
  - **No 5-year minimum** where the employment ends because of death or disablement, or because a fixed term expires. Death cases are paid pro rata.
  - **Fixed-term employees:** eligible after at least 1 year of service under the contract, paid pro rata. Under Central Rule 33(1), a remaining period of more than 6 months but less than 1 year counts as one more year.
  - **Working journalists:** 3 years.
- **Ceiling:** "such amount as may be notified" (s.53(3)). The Labour Ministry FAQs (30 Dec 2025) say it is **currently ₹20 lakh**. Under the old Act this was S.O. 1420(E) of 29 Mar 2018.
  - One web page claimed a private-sector rise to ₹25 lakh under "S.O. 1234(E)" from 1 Apr 2026. That claim is not corroborated by any official or major source, cites the repealed 1972 Act, and should be **treated as false**.
  - Central government employees have a separate, higher cap under their own pension rules. It is not relevant to the private sector.
- **"Wages" for gratuity:** SS Code s.2(88), the same definition and 50% rule as 1.1. The final SS Central Rules dropped the draft's extra exclusions.
  - Ministry Additional FAQs (Mar 2026): the new definition applies from 21 Nov 2025. Earlier service fell under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, but the amount is computed on the last drawn wage under the new definition (SCC summary).
- **Payment:**
  - Due within 30 days from the date it becomes payable (s.56(3)). Delay attracts simple interest at a rate not exceeding the notified rate (s.56(4)).
  - Central Rules: nomination in Form III within 90 days of completing one year; claim in Form IV; employer decision in Form V within 15 days.
  - Forfeiture is possible for wilful damage, riotous conduct or offences involving moral turpitude (s.53(6)).
- **Tax exemption, private sector (do not compute in toolkit):** the long-standing exemption cap is ₹20 lakh. Under the 1961 Act it was set by S.O. 1420(E) (29 Mar 2018) for employees covered by the Gratuity Act, and S.O. 1213(E) (8 Mar 2019) for others.
  - From tax year 2026-27, exemption sits in the **Income-tax Act, 2025, s.19(1) Table**:
    - Entry 5: gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972.
    - Entry 6: other gratuity, at the lowest of the actual amount, a notified limit and half a month's average salary per completed year.
  - Entry 5 still cross-refers to the repealed 1972 Act, and I could not find a fresh notification of the ₹20 lakh figure under the 2025 Act. **Treat the ₹20 lakh tax cap as "reported, confirm with a tax adviser".**
- **Effective:** 21 Nov 2025.
- **Law:** SS Code ss.53, 54, 56 and 2(88); IR Code s.2(o)(c); SS (Central) Rules 2026, Rules 31–33.
- **Sources:**
  - [Courtbook: SS Code s.53](https://courtbook.in/code-on-social-security-2020/section-53)
  - [Courtbook: SS Code s.54](https://courtbook.in/code-on-social-security-2020/section-54)
  - [Courtbook: SS Code s.56](https://courtbook.in/code-on-social-security-2020/section-56)
  - [IR Code (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22040/1/aa202035.pdf)
  - [TaxClue: Section 53 SS Code, payment of gratuity (cites Rule 33(1))](https://taxclue.in/blog/section-53-social-security-code-2020-payment-of-gratuity/)
  - [Mondaq: Notified SS Code Central Rules 2026, what employers need to know](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/employee-rights-labour-relations/1792872/notified-social-security-code-central-rules-2026-what-employers-need-to-know)
  - [SCC Online: Labour Ministry FAQs (₹20 lakh ceiling)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/01/03/labour-ministry-issued-faqs-on-the-labour-codes/)

## 3. EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)

- **Rule:** In covered establishments (20 or more employees), the employee and the employer each contribute 12% of PF wages. Part of the employer's share goes to the pension scheme (EPS), capped at the wage ceiling. Mandatory coverage now extends to wages up to ₹25,000 a month.
- **Numbers (from 17 Sep 2026):**

  | Item | Rate | Base | Monthly amount at the ₹25,000 ceiling |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Employee contribution | 12% | PF wages (at least up to the ceiling; may be on full wages) | ₹3,000 |
  | Employer contribution, total | 12% | same as above | ₹3,000 |
  | → of which EPS (pension) | 8.33% | wages capped at the ceiling | ₹2,083 |
  | → of which EPF (balance) | 12% minus EPS (about 3.67% at the ceiling) | — | ₹917 |
  | EDLI (insurance) | 0.5% employer | capped at the ceiling | ₹125 |
  | Admin charges | 0.5% employer | EPF wages; establishment minimum ₹500 a month (₹75 if it has no contributing member) | ₹125 |

  - **Lower rate:** 10% instead of 12% applies to establishments under IBC resolution or repayment plans, and to jute, beedi, brick, coir (other than spinning) and guar-gum units (S.O. 3582(E), 1 Jul 2026).
  - **Government EPS support:** stays at 1.16% of wages up to ₹15,000 (EPFO FAQ). This is not an employer cost.
- **Wage ceiling history:**
  - ₹15,000 from Sep 2014 (re-notified under the SS Code by S.O. 2702(E), 29 May 2026).
  - **₹25,000 from 17 Sep 2026** (S.O. 5109(E), 17 Sep 2026, under SS Code s.2(89)). The Union Cabinet approved it on 16 Sep 2026.
  - **September 2026 was split:** 1–16 Sep on ₹15,000 and 17–30 Sep on ₹25,000, in one ECR due 15 Oct 2026.
- **Who can opt out ("excluded employee"):** an employee whose wages, at the time they would otherwise become a member, exceed the ceiling (now ₹25,000). Being "excluded" means they are not required to join.
  - Existing members cannot opt out.
  - Excluded employees may join voluntarily with the employer's consent.
  - Existing excluded employees whose wages are below ₹25,000 had to be enrolled from 17 Sep 2026.
  - **EPS membership** is available only to employees whose wages did not exceed the ceiling at joining, or on the date the new ceiling took effect. Employees who were excluded from EPS only because they earned ₹15,000–25,000 must be enrolled in EPS from 17 Sep 2026.
  - International workers have no ceiling.
- **Schemes in force:** EPF Scheme 2026 (G.S.R. 525(E)), EPS 2026 (G.S.R. 527(E)) and EDLI Scheme 2026 (G.S.R. 526(E)), all dated 29 Jun 2026 and in force on publication. Corrigenda G.S.R. 703–705(E) were issued on 4 Aug 2026. Rate notifications are dated 1 Jul 2026; the EPF rate notification is deemed effective from 21 Nov 2025.
- **"PF wages":** SS Code s.2(88) wages, which can be more than basic + DA (see 1.1).
- **Law:** SS Code ss.2(88), 2(89), 16 (default 10%, raised to 12% by notification) and First Schedule (20+ employees); EPF Scheme 2026 paras 2(f) and 19.

## 4. ESI (Employees' State Insurance)

- **Rule:** Employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month (₹25,000 for persons with disability) in covered establishments are insured. The employee pays 0.75% of wages and the employer 3.25%, each rounded up to the next rupee.
- **Numbers:**
  - **Wage ceiling:** ₹21,000 a month, or ₹25,000 for persons with disability. Unchanged; no SS Code Chapter IV ceiling notification yet. The Ministry FAQ (16 Mar 2026) says the ₹21,000 threshold continues pending revised rules.
  - **Rates:** employer 3.25%, employee 0.75%, "rounded to the next higher rupee" (SS (Central) Rules 2026, Rule 19(1)).
  - **Low-wage exemption:** employees with an average daily wage of ₹176 or less pay no employee share; the employer share is still payable.
  - **Contribution periods:** 1 Apr–30 Sep and 1 Oct–31 Mar. Someone covered at the start of a period stays covered until it ends, even if wages rise above the ceiling.
- **Changes under the SS Code:**
  - Coverage applies across India.
  - Voluntary for establishments with fewer than 10 employees.
  - Mandatory where even one employee works in hazardous or life-threatening processes.
  - The s.2(88) wages definition (with the 50% rule) governs contributions from 21 Nov 2025.
  - Insured women qualify for maternity benefit if contributions were payable for 70 days in the two preceding contribution periods. The benefit is 26 weeks, or 12 weeks for adopting or commissioning mothers. A confinement "medical bonus" of ₹15,000 per case is payable where ESI facilities are unavailable (Rule 22(3)–(4)).
  - Old ESI regulations continue until 20 Nov 2026 under s.164(2)(b). **Replacement ESI scheme and regulations, and any new ceiling, were not notified as of the latest sources (Oct 2026).**
- **Law:** SS Code Chapter IV, s.2(89) and s.164; SS (Central) Rules 2026, Rules 19 and 22.

## 6. Maternity benefit

- **Rule:** A woman who has worked at least 80 days in the 12 months before her expected delivery gets up to 26 weeks of paid maternity benefit, or 12 weeks if she already has two or more surviving children. Adopting and commissioning mothers get 12 weeks.
- **Numbers:**
  - **Leave:**
    - 26 weeks, with no more than 8 before the expected delivery.
    - 12 weeks if she has 2 or more surviving children, with no more than 6 before delivery.
    - Adoptive or commissioning mother: 12 weeks from the day the child is handed over. **Since 17 Mar 2026 this applies regardless of the child's age**; the Supreme Court struck down the "below 3 months" condition in *Hamsaanandini Nanduri v. Union of India*, 2026 INSC 246.
  - **Pay:** at the average daily wage, which cannot be below the minimum wage.
  - **Other paid leave:** 6 weeks for miscarriage or medical termination; 2 weeks for tubectomy; up to 1 extra month for pregnancy-related illness.
  - **Medical bonus (employer-paid):** ₹3,500, or such amount as the Centre notifies, if the employer provides no free pre- and post-natal care (s.64). **No notification changing ₹3,500 was found.** Do not confuse this with the ESI ₹15,000 confinement amount in topic 4.
  - **Nursing breaks:** 2 a day until the child is 15 months old (s.66). Under the Central Rules each break is 15 minutes, plus up to 15 minutes of travel time.
  - **Crèche:** required for 50 or more employees (s.67), for children under 6, generally within 1 km. The mother may visit 4 times a day.
    - Under the Central Rules the crèche serves employees of either gender.
    - An allowance of at least ₹500 a month per child (up to 2 children) can replace the crèche by agreement. PRS questions whether the Rules can allow this substitution.
  - **Work from home:** may be allowed after the maternity period on mutually agreed terms, where the nature of work permits (s.60).
  - **Written notice:** women must be told of these benefits in writing and electronically at appointment (s.67).
- **Changes under the SS Code:** the Maternity Benefit Act 1961 is now Chapter VI of the SS Code (in force 21 Nov 2025). It covers factories, mines, plantations and shops/establishments with 10 or more employees. Other changes are the crèche-allowance option, the gross-misconduct list in the Rules, and the Supreme Court's 2026 ruling on adoption.
- **Law:** SS Code ss.60, 64, 65, 66, 67; SS (Central) Rules 2026 (Rule 37 for crèche).

## 7. Paternity leave

- **Rule:** No central law requires private employers to give paternity leave. It is a matter of company policy, subject to any state-specific rule. In March 2026 the Supreme Court urged the Union government to legislate it as a social-security benefit, but no law had been enacted as of the latest sources.
- **Central government comparison:**
  - Male central government servants with fewer than 2 surviving children get **15 days' paid paternity leave**. It can be taken up to 15 days before, or within 6 months after, the child's delivery, and it lapses if not used (CCS (Leave) Rules 1972, Rule 43-A).
  - **15 days** are also available on valid adoption of a child under 1 year old, within 6 months of the adoption (Rule 43-AA).

## 8. Leave norms (casual and sick leave, holidays)

- **Rule:** There is no national minimum of casual or sick leave for private-sector employees.
  - The OSH Code gives only annual leave with wages to "workers" (see 1.3).
  - Casual and sick leave come from state Shops & Establishments Acts, standing orders where they apply, and company policy.
  - Paid national and festival holidays come from state laws.
  - ESI sickness benefit is a cash benefit for insured persons, not a leave entitlement.
- **Karnataka (Karnataka Shops & Commercial Establishments Act, 1961):**
  - Annual leave: 1 day per 20 days worked for adults, 1 per 15 for young persons. There is no 240-day minimum since a 1997 amendment (s.15(1)).
  - Carry-forward: up to 45 days. It was raised from 30 by the Second Amendment Act 2020, published Feb 2021.
  - Sickness or accident leave: up to 12 days with wages per 12 months of service, also usable for "any other reasonable cause" (s.15(3)). There is no separate casual-leave clause.
  - **Holidays:** 10 paid holidays a year under the Karnataka Industrial Establishments (National and Festival Holidays) Act, 1963 (s.3, as amended). Five are fixed: 26 Jan, 1 May, 15 Aug, 2 Oct and 1 Nov. The employer picks the other five from the scheduled festivals. One law-firm page says "at least 5"; I treat 10 as current, with Medium confidence.
  - **New caveat:** Karnataka Act 38 of 2026 (in force 4 Sep 2026) excludes establishments with 10 or more workers that are registered under the OSH Code from the S&CE Act. K S & K and AZB read this as exclusion from the whole Act; SCC Online describes it as registration relief. Where an establishment is excluded, its leave rules fall back on the OSH Code (workers) and company policy.
- **Maharashtra (Maharashtra Shops & Establishments (RECS) Act, 2017, s.18):**
  - Weekly holiday with wages.
  - **8 days' casual leave** a year. It lapses at year-end; one source says it is credited quarterly.
  - Earned leave: 1 day per 20 days worked for workers with 240 days in the year. Workers with at least 3 months' service get 5 days per 60 days worked.
  - Accumulation up to **45 days**, with encashment of the excess if leave is refused.
  - **8 paid festival holidays:** 26 Jan, 1 May, 15 Aug and 2 Oct, plus 4 agreed before the year starts. Work on these days earns double wages plus a substitute day off.
  - Applies to establishments with 10 or more workers. A **1 Oct 2025 Ordinance** raised this to 20 workers, allowed 10-hour days and a 12-hour spread-over, and raised the overtime cap to 144 hours per quarter. The replacing Bill was introduced on 8 Dec 2025, deemed effective 1 Oct 2025. **Its final enactment status was not verified.**

## 9. Notice period

- **Rule:** No Indian law sets a general resignation notice period for private-sector employees; it comes from the employment contract. Statute adds employer-side notice only in specific cases.
- **Statutory overlays:**
  1. **Retrenchment of a "worker"** with at least 1 year's continuous service (IR Code s.70) requires:
     - one month's written notice stating reasons, or wages in lieu;
     - retrenchment compensation of 15 days' average pay per completed year (part-years over 6 months count as a year);
     - notice to the government.
     - In addition, the employer pays 15 days' last drawn wages into the worker re-skilling fund within 10 days (s.83).
     - Establishments with **300 or more workers** need prior government permission (Chapter X).
     - "Worker" excludes managerial/administrative staff and supervisors earning above ₹18,000 a month (s.2(zr)).
  2. **Standing orders** (establishments with 300 or more workers; IR Code s.28 and the Model Standing Orders 2026): one month's notice or wages in lieu to terminate a permanent worker. No notice is due when a temporary, probationary or fixed-term contract ends or is not renewed. The Model Standing Orders set no resignation-notice period for workers. Probation is 6 months, extendable by 3.
  3. **State S&E Acts may require employer notice.** Example: Karnataka S&CE Act s.39 requires one month's notice or pay in lieu to dismiss an employee with at least 6 months' continuous service, except for misconduct proved at an enquiry. This applies only to establishments still within that Act (see topic 8).
- **Garden leave and notice buy-out:** neither is regulated by statute; both are purely contractual. Post-employment non-competes are generally void under s.27 of the Indian Contract Act 1872. Restrictions during employment, including a garden-leave notice period, are more likely to be enforced.

## 10. Weekly off, working hours, overtime

- **Rule:** A worker may not be made to work more than 8 hours a day, 48 hours a week or 6 days a week. Overtime needs the worker's consent and is paid at twice the ordinary rate.
- **Numbers:**
  - **Daily:** 8 hours (OSH s.25(1)(a)).
  - **Weekly:** 48 hours (OSH Central Rules, Rule 64(1)).
  - **Days:** no more than 6 in a week (OSH s.26). Compensatory holidays are due where an exemption removes the weekly holiday.
  - **Code on Wages Central Rules:** weekly rest day, normally Sunday; no more than 10 consecutive days without a full rest day.
  - **Rest interval:** no more than 5 hours of continuous work without a break of at least 30 minutes (S.O. 2517(E), 13 May 2026, under OSH s.25(1)(b)).
  - **Spread-over:** not verified for the central sphere. Under state law, for example, Maharashtra allows 12 hours after the 2025 Ordinance.
  - **Overtime:**
    - Rate: twice the ordinary rate of wages (OSH s.27). The Code on Wages s.14 says "not less than twice".
    - Trigger: work beyond 8 hours a day for daily-wage staff, or beyond 48 hours a week for others.
    - Timing: paid at the end of the wage period.
    - Rounding: 15–30 minutes counts as 30 minutes; more than 30 minutes counts as 1 hour.
    - Daily rate: monthly wage ÷ 26 (OSH Central Rule 69).
    - Cap: 144 hours per quarter (Central Rules).
  - **Minimum-wage maths:** hourly = daily ÷ 8; monthly = daily × 26 (Code on Wages Central Rules).
  - **Flexible schedules:** for example, longer days in a 4-day week are permitted within the 48-hour cap. They are not mandated.
- **Applies to:** "workers" in OSH establishments with 10 or more workers. State S&E Act limits continue to apply, for example 9 hours a day in Karnataka and in Maharashtra before the Ordinance.
- **Sources:**
  - [OSH Code (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22041/1/2020-37.pdf)

## 11. Income tax on salary, FY 2026-27 (note only; the toolkit will not compute tax)

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** came into force on **1 April 2026** and replaces the Income-tax Act, 1961. Parliament passed it on 12 Aug 2025 and the President assented on 21 Aug 2025. It is operationalised by the **Income-tax Rules, 2026**, notified by CBDT on 20 Mar 2026. The old "previous year / assessment year" pair is replaced by a single **"tax year"**, so FY 2026-27 is tax year 2026-27. Income for FY 2025-26 and earlier is still assessed under the 1961 Act. Under **section 202** of the 2025 Act, the **new tax regime is the default** for individuals and HUFs. A salaried individual without business income can opt for the old regime when filing the return of income for that tax year; those with business income must opt by the return due date and can withdraw only once. Slab rates are set out in s.202(1) on the Income Tax Department site. PeopleCraft should link to that page rather than reproduce or compute them.

### (a) Gratuity: IMPLEMENT (with the listed exclusions)
Inputs:

```

years_for_amount = Y_full + (1 if (M_rem > 6) or (M_rem == 6 and D_rem > 0) else 0)
```
