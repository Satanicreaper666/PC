## {{company}} policy on the prevention of sexual harassment

Applies from {{effective_date}}, at {{locations}}.

### 1. Our commitment

{{company}} does not tolerate sexual harassment. Everyone has the right to work with dignity, free from harassment. This policy follows the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (the POSH Act). It protects every woman at our workplaces, whether she is an employee, a contract or agency worker, an intern or trainee, a visitor or a client. We also expect everyone to treat colleagues of every gender with respect, and we will act on any complaint of harassment under our code of conduct.

### 2. What sexual harassment is

Sexual harassment is any unwelcome act or behaviour of a sexual nature, directly or by implication, including:

- physical contact and advances;
- a demand or request for sexual favours;
- sexually coloured remarks, jokes or messages, in person, by phone, chat, email or social media;
- showing pornography;
- any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

It also includes, when connected with such conduct, an implied or explicit promise of better treatment or threat of worse treatment at work, interfering with someone's work, or creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile work environment.

### 3. Where it applies

The workplace includes our offices, plants and sites, any place visited because of work (including client sites, travel, hotels and transport we provide), work events and offsites, and online spaces used for work.

### 4. The Internal Committee

Our Internal Committee receives and inquires into complaints. Its members are:

- **Presiding Officer:** {{presiding_officer}}
- **Members:** {{ic_members}}

At least half the members are women, and one is an external member from an organisation committed to the cause of women. The committee can be reached at {{ic_email}}. Its members' names and contacts are displayed at every workplace.

If the complaint is against the employer, or a workplace has no Internal Committee, a complaint can be made to the Local Committee set up by the District Officer.

Local Committee contact: {{district_officer}}

Complaints can also be made online through the government's SHe-Box portal, which sends them to the right committee.

### 5. How to complain

- Write to the Internal Committee within three months of the incident, or of the last incident if there was more than one. The committee can allow three more months if something stopped you complaining sooner.
- You can use the company's complaint form, write a letter or email, or ask a committee member to help you write it.
- If you can't complain yourself because of physical or mental incapacity, or after death, a relative, friend, co-worker or another person allowed by the Act can complain for you.

### 6. Help while the inquiry runs

On your written request, the committee can recommend that we transfer you or the other person, give you up to three months' paid leave (in addition to your leave entitlement), or stop the other person from reporting on your work or writing your appraisal. We will act on these recommendations.

### 7. Conciliation

Before an inquiry, and only if you ask for it, the committee can try to settle the matter through conciliation. No money can be part of a settlement. If a settlement is reached and not kept, you can ask the committee to hold an inquiry.

### 8. The inquiry

- The committee will send a copy of the complaint to the other person and give both of you a fair chance to be heard, to give evidence and to question each other's evidence.
- Lawyers do not represent either side in the inquiry.
- The committee completes the inquiry within 90 days and gives its report to the employer and both of you within 10 days of finishing.
- If the complaint is proved, the committee recommends action, which can include a written apology, a warning, a reprimand, withholding a promotion or increment, counselling, community service or termination, and compensation to be paid by the other person. We will act on the recommendations within 60 days.

### 9. Confidentiality

The complaint, the names and addresses of the people involved and of witnesses, the inquiry and the action taken are confidential. They are not published or shared with the media or the public. Anyone who breaks this confidentiality faces disciplinary action. Information about the justice secured may be shared without names or details that could identify anyone.

### 10. False complaints

A complaint that cannot be proved is not a false complaint. Action is taken only if the committee finds that a complaint was made knowing it to be false, or with malice, or with forged or misleading documents, after an inquiry.

### 11. Protection from retaliation

No one will be victimised for making a complaint in good faith, for helping someone complain, or for being a witness. Retaliation is misconduct, and we will act on it.

### 12. Appeals

Either person can appeal against the committee's recommendations, or their non-implementation, to the appellate authority under the service rules or the law, within 90 days of the recommendations.

### 13. Awareness

We hold awareness sessions {{training}}, and training for committee members. The committee prepares an annual report each calendar year and sends it to the company and the District Officer.

Questions about this policy can be sent in confidence to {{ic_email}}.
