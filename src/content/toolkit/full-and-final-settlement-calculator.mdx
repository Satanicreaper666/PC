  - q: What is included in a full and final settlement?
    a: Salary for the days worked in the last month, encashment of unused leave, gratuity if eligible, bonus or other dues, and notice pay if the employer pays in lieu of notice, less agreed recoveries such as loans or a notice shortfall.
  - q: Is PF part of the full and final settlement?
    a: No. The employer deducts the employee's PF on the final month's wages and files the last contribution, but the employee withdraws or transfers PF by making a claim to EPFO.

The example is for someone in Bengaluru who joined on 1 April 2019 and resigned with a last working day of Friday 4 December 2026, on a gross salary of ₹93,000 a month and wages (as the Labour Codes define them) of ₹60,000, on the new tax regime.

- **Final salary:** gross monthly pay ÷ the days in the month × the days payable. Payroll may use 30 or 26 instead; the calculator lets you choose. ₹93,000 ÷ 31 × 4 = ₹12,000.
- **Leave encashment:** wages ÷ 26 (or 30) × unused leave days, as the [leave encashment calculator](/toolkit/leave-encashment-calculator/) explains. ₹60,000 ÷ 26 × 18 = ₹41,538.
- **Gratuity:** if the employee has <Fact id="gratuity.minYears" /> of continuous service, or a fixed-term employee has <Fact id="gratuity.fixedTermMinYears" />, as the [gratuity calculator](/toolkit/gratuity-calculator/) explains. Seven years and eight months counts as 8 years: ₹2,76,923.
- **Deductions from pay:** the employee's PF on the final month's wages (12% of ₹7,742 earned in 4 days = ₹929), ESI if they are covered, professional tax at the state's slab on the final month's salary (nil in Karnataka on ₹12,000), and TDS. Her taxable salary for the year is about ₹7.6 lakh, inside the ₹12 lakh rebate, so the TDS is nil.
- **Other dues and recoveries:** you enter these. They include bonus, reimbursements, arrears, notice pay, loans and the agreed value of assets not returned.

## Retrenchment and closure

When a **worker** with at least a year's continuous service is retrenched, the Industrial Relations Code requires, at the time of retrenchment:

- <Fact id="notice.retrenchmentNoticeMonths" />'s written notice with the reasons, or wages for the notice period in lieu (section 70(a));
- compensation of <Fact id="notice.retrenchmentCompensationDays" /> for every completed year of continuous service, and for any part of a year over six months (section 70(b));
- a separate payment by the employer of <Fact id="notice.reskillingFundDays" /> into the worker re-skilling fund, which credits it to the worker (section 83).

Closure pays the same compensation, as if the worker were retrenched (section 75). When the undertaking closes because of circumstances beyond the employer's control, the compensation can't be more than <Fact id="notice.closureCompensationCapMonths" />; losses, unsold stock and the end of a lease don't count. Establishments with <Fact id="notice.layoffPermissionWorkers" /> or more need the government's permission first.

Managerial and administrative staff, and supervisors above the wage limit, aren't workers, so this statutory compensation doesn't apply to them. Pay what their contract, policy or separation agreement says, and enter it as **severance**.

## Income tax on the settlement

The settlement is part of the year's salary, so TDS is worked out on the whole tax year with you, not on the 12-month projection payroll used while the employee was working. The calculator:

1. adds the salary already paid this tax year (or assumes the monthly gross for each earlier month) to the taxable part of the settlement;
2. leaves out what is exempt: gratuity up to the lifetime limit, leave encashment on leaving within its limits, and a worker's retrenchment compensation up to the least of the amount, the 15-days formula and the notified limit (reported as ₹5 lakh), which any ex gratia paid with it shares;
3. applies the regime's standard deduction, slabs, rebate, surcharge and 4% cess;
4. subtracts the TDS already deducted this year. If that is more than the year's tax, it deducts nothing: the employee claims the excess back in the return.

In the retrenchment example, ₹5.9 lakh of gratuity, leave and compensation is exempt. The ₹2 lakh ex gratia is taxable, but the year's taxable salary is still only about ₹10.2 lakh, so the new regime's rebate leaves no tax. Severance paid to someone who isn't a worker is taxable salary in full. The exemption limits were carried over from the Income-tax Act, 1961, and the "Rates and limits" box under the calculator shows how firmly each is sourced: confirm them with your tax adviser, and ask the employee whether they used any gratuity or leave exemption with an earlier employer. The TDS handbook explains the method in [joiners and leavers](/handbooks/tds/joiners-leavers-and-transfers/).

<Callout type="law" title="What the law says">
The Code on Wages, 2019 (section 17(2)) says wages due to an employee who is removed, dismissed, retrenched, resigns or loses the job because the establishment closes must be paid within <Fact id="wages.separationPayDays" />. For workers under the OSH Code, unused leave is paid within <Fact id="leave.exitPaymentWorkingDays" /> of discharge, dismissal or quitting, and within <Fact id="leave.retirementPaymentMonths" /> of retirement or death. Gratuity is due within <Fact id="gratuity.paymentDays" /> of becoming payable under the Code on Social Security.
</Callout>

The Code counts the two working days from the resignation itself. It is commonly read as the date the resignation takes effect, the last working day, but there is no official clarification yet. The two-working-day rule does not name retirement, death or the end of a fixed-term contract; for those, pay on the normal pay date (<Fact id="wages.monthlyPayDay" /> for monthly-paid staff) or sooner if your policy says so.
