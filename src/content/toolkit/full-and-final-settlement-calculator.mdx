## How the amount is worked out

The example is for someone who joined on 1 April 2019 and resigned with a last working day of Friday 4 December 2026, on a gross salary of ₹93,000 a month and wages (as the Labour Codes define them) of ₹60,000.

- **Final salary:** gross monthly pay ÷ the days in the month × the days payable. Payroll may use 30 or 26 instead; the calculator lets you choose. ₹93,000 ÷ 31 × 4 = ₹12,000.
- **Leave encashment:** wages ÷ 26 (or 30) × unused leave days, as the [leave encashment calculator](/toolkit/leave-encashment-calculator/) explains. ₹60,000 ÷ 26 × 18 = ₹41,538.
- **Gratuity:** if the employee has <Fact id="gratuity.minYears" /> of continuous service, or a fixed-term employee has <Fact id="gratuity.fixedTermMinYears" />, as the [gratuity calculator](/toolkit/gratuity-calculator/) explains. Seven years and eight months counts as 8 years: ₹2,76,923.
- **Other dues and recoveries:** you enter these. They include bonus, reimbursements, arrears, notice pay, loans and the agreed value of assets not returned.

<Callout type="warning" title="Notice pay is set by the contract">
How much is recovered for notice not served, or paid in lieu of notice, depends on the contract: which pay it counts and how many days make a month. Work it out from the contract and enter the amount. Recover only what the employee agreed to or the law allows.
</Callout>

<Callout type="law" title="What the law says">
The Code on Wages, 2019 (section 17(2)) says wages due to an employee who is removed, dismissed, retrenched, resigns or loses the job because the establishment closes must be paid within <Fact id="wages.separationPayDays" />. For workers under the OSH Code, unused leave is paid within <Fact id="leave.exitPaymentWorkingDays" /> of discharge, dismissal or quitting, and within <Fact id="leave.retirementPaymentMonths" /> of retirement or death. Gratuity is due within <Fact id="gratuity.paymentDays" /> of becoming payable under the Code on Social Security.
</Callout>

The Code counts the two working days from the resignation itself. It is commonly read as the date the resignation takes effect, the last working day, but there is no official clarification yet. The two-working-day rule does not name retirement, death or the end of a fixed-term contract; for those, pay on the normal pay date (<Fact id="wages.monthlyPayDay" /> for monthly-paid staff) or sooner if your policy says so.
