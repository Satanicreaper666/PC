faq:
  - q: What is natural justice in a disciplinary inquiry?
    a: Two ideas. The employee must know the case against them and have a fair chance to answer it, and the decision must be made by someone without a stake in the outcome. In practice that means a specific charge, access to the evidence, a chance to question witnesses and give evidence, an independent inquiry officer and a reasoned decision.
  - q: Is a domestic inquiry needed before every warning?
    a: No. For a minor lapse, a show-cause notice and a fair hearing of the reply are usually enough before a warning. A full inquiry is needed before a major penalty such as withholding increments, demotion, discharge or dismissal, especially when the facts are disputed.
  - q: How long should a disciplinary process take?
    a: As short as fairness allows. If the employee is suspended, the Industrial Relations Code expects the inquiry to finish ordinarily within 90 days, and the subsistence allowance rises after that if the delay isn't the employee's fault.

## The checks a tribunal will make

Work through these before you issue the final order. Each "no" is a reason the order could be set aside.

| Check | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| The charge was specific | Each charge names the act, the date, the place and the rule broken |
| The employee saw the evidence | Copies or inspection of every document relied on, in time to prepare |
| The inquiry officer was independent | Not a witness, not the investigator, not the person who decides the penalty |
| The employee could answer | They could question each witness, give evidence and call witnesses, with a helper the rules allow |
| The record is complete | Each day's proceedings written down and signed, in a language the employee understands |
| The findings follow the evidence | Reasons for each finding, dealing with the defence, using only evidence produced at the inquiry |
| The employee saw the report | A copy of the report and time to comment before the penalty was decided |
| The penalty is proportionate | Reasons given, weighing seriousness, record, service, admission and past cases |
| Rules were followed | Standing orders or policy steps and time limits met; suspension allowance paid |

## Choosing a proportionate penalty

Use only the penalties your standing orders or policy list. Start from how serious the proved act was, then move up or down for the factors below, and write your reasons in the order.

| How serious | Examples | Usual range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | Occasional lateness, a first breach of a minor rule, careless but harmless mistakes | Counselling, oral or written warning |
| Serious | Repeated minor lapses after warnings, negligence that caused loss, refusing a reasonable instruction | Final warning, withholding an increment, a short suspension as a penalty |
| Gross | Theft, fraud, violence, a serious safety breach that put people at risk, sexual harassment proved by the Internal Committee | Demotion, discharge or dismissal |

**Factors that move it down:** long service with a clean record, an early and honest admission, provocation, a genuine misunderstanding of the rule, and a weak system that made the lapse easy.

**Factors that move it up:** earlier warnings for the same kind of conduct, a position of trust, deliberate concealment, and harm to customers, colleagues or safety.

<Callout type="warning" title="Treat like cases alike">
Before deciding, look at what you did in similar cases in the last few years. A harsher penalty for one person than for others who did the same thing is hard to defend, and can look like discrimination.
</Callout>

## After the order

- **Warning or lesser penalty:** put the order on file, revoke any suspension and pay any wages due for the suspension period as the order says.
- **Discharge or dismissal:** pay the final wages within <Fact id="wages.separationPayDays" /> of the last day, with leave encashment and gratuity as the law requires. Gratuity can be forfeited only in the cases the Code on Social Security allows, by a reasoned order. Use the [full and final settlement calculator](/toolkit/full-and-final-settlement-calculator/) and its settlement statement.
- **Appeal:** decide it promptly, by someone not involved, with reasons.
