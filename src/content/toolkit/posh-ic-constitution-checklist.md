## Decide where you need a committee

The POSH Act requires an Internal Committee at every office or administrative unit with 10 or more employees. Where a workplace has fewer than 10, complaints go to the Local Committee set up by the District Officer.

- [ ] List every office, branch and unit, with its headcount
- [ ] Mark each one with 10 or more employees as needing its own committee
- [ ] Note smaller offices, and tell their staff how to reach the Local Committee for their district

## Choose the members

- [ ] Presiding Officer: a woman employed at a senior level at that workplace (or, if there is none, from another office or unit of the same employer)
- [ ] At least two members from among employees, preferably people committed to the cause of women, or with experience in social work or legal knowledge
- [ ] One external member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment
- [ ] At least half of all members are women
- [ ] Each member's term set at no more than three years from the date of nomination
- [ ] External member's fee and expenses agreed in writing before the first case
- [ ] Each member has agreed in writing to serve and to keep proceedings confidential

## Make it official

- [ ] Written order constituting the committee issued by the employer, with members' names, roles, contact details and term
- [ ] Order displayed at a conspicuous place in every workplace it covers, and on the intranet
- [ ] Penalties for sexual harassment displayed alongside the order
- [ ] POSH policy written, approved and shared with every employee, including new joiners
- [ ] A clear way to complain published: the committee's email address, the complaint form and what happens next

## Train people

- [ ] Committee members trained before their first case: the inquiry process, timelines, fair hearing for both sides, confidentiality and report writing
- [ ] Awareness sessions for all employees held at regular intervals
- [ ] Managers taught how to respond when someone raises a concern, and how to pass it to the committee without delay

## Run it properly

- [ ] At least three members, including the Presiding Officer, present for inquiry proceedings
- [ ] Timelines tracked for every case: inquiry finished within 90 days, report issued within 10 days of finishing, employer action within 60 days of the report
- [ ] Interim relief considered whenever the complainant asks for it in writing
- [ ] Case records kept confidential and stored with restricted access
- [ ] Annual report prepared for each calendar year and sent to the employer and the District Officer
- [ ] Number of cases and how they were resolved included in the organisation's annual report (for companies, the Board's report)
- [ ] Vacancies filled promptly and terms renewed before they expire
