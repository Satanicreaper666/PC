## Registrations and coverage

Many duties start at a headcount threshold, so check each office and each state you employ people in.

- [ ] Registered with EPFO if you employ 20 or more people, or registered voluntarily
- [ ] Registered with ESIC if you have 10 or more employees, with everyone earning up to {{fact:esi.wageCeiling}} ({{fact:esi.wageCeilingPwd|short}} for a person with disability) insured
- [ ] Registered for professional tax in every state that levies it, and deducting it at that state's rates
- [ ] Labour welfare fund contributions paid on each state's schedule, where the state has a fund
- [ ] Every office registered under the state's Shops and Establishments Act or the OSH Code, as that state requires

## Wages and pay

- [ ] Every employee paid at least the applicable minimum wage, which now covers all employees, not only listed jobs
- [ ] Monthly wages paid by the 7th of the following month
- [ ] Salary structures checked against the 50% rule: if pay items left out of "wages" (such as HRA and conveyance) exceed half of total pay, the excess is treated as wages for PF and gratuity
- [ ] No difference in pay on the ground of gender for the same or similar work
- [ ] Overtime worked only with the employee's consent and paid at twice the ordinary rate of wages
- [ ] Statutory bonus paid within eight months of the end of the accounting year (by 30 November for an April to March year)
- [ ] Fixed-term employees given the same pay and benefits as comparable permanent employees

## PF, ESI, gratuity and maternity

Old ESI regulations continue until 20 November 2026 while new ones are awaited, so re-check the ESI ceiling and due dates after that date.

- [ ] PF return (ECR) filed and contributions paid by the 15th of the following month
- [ ] Everyone earning up to the PF wage ceiling enrolled in PF, including people newly covered when the ceiling rose to ₹25,000 a month on 17 September 2026
- [ ] ESI contributions paid by the 15th of the following month
- [ ] PF and gratuity nominations on record for every employee
- [ ] Gratuity liability provided for, including fixed-term employees, who qualify after one year of service
- [ ] Maternity benefit given in full ({{fact:maternity.weeks}}; {{fact:maternity.weeksTwoChildren}} for a woman with two or more surviving children, and for adopting or commissioning mothers), and women told about it in writing at joining

## Tax on salary

- [ ] TDS on salary worked out under the tax regime each employee chose (the new regime is the default), using their claims in Form 124 (earlier Form 12BB)
- [ ] TDS deposited by the 7th of the following month, and by 30 April for March
- [ ] Quarterly TDS statements filed by 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May, and salary TDS certificates issued by 15 June (Form 130 for tax year 2026-27 onwards; Form 16 for earlier years)

## Contracts and records

- [ ] Every employee has an appointment letter (a legal duty once you have 10 or more workers), including people who joined before the Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025
- [ ] Employee handbook or policy manual shared with every employee
- [ ] Confidentiality agreements signed where the role handles confidential information
- [ ] Background checks done only with the candidate's written consent, and limited to what the role needs
- [ ] PAN collected for TDS, emergency contacts on file, and identity documents stored securely with limited access
- [ ] Wage, attendance and leave registers kept in the form the Labour Codes and your state's rules require

## POSH

The POSH Act applies to every workplace. Where there are fewer than 10 employees, complaints go to the Local Committee set up by the District Officer. Failing to set up an Internal Committee can be fined up to ₹50,000.

- [ ] Internal Committee set up at every office or unit with 10 or more employees
- [ ] Presiding Officer is a woman employed at a senior level, and at least half of all members are women
- [ ] One external member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues of sexual harassment
- [ ] POSH policy circulated, and the committee order and the penalties for sexual harassment displayed at the workplace
- [ ] Awareness sessions for employees and training for committee members held at regular intervals
- [ ] Committee's annual report for each calendar year sent to the employer and the District Officer
- [ ] Employees told they can also complain through the government's SHe-Box portal, which sends complaints to the Internal Committee or the Local Committee

## Contract labour, standing orders and other thresholds

These duties start at a headcount. Count contract and agency workers as the Code you are checking says.

- [ ] Contractors who supply 50 or more contract workers hold a licence under the OSH Code, and you checked it before work started
- [ ] As principal employer, you check every month that contract workers are paid in full and on time, and get the welfare facilities the OSH Code requires; if a contractor doesn't pay, the principal employer can be made to pay
- [ ] Contract workers are not used for core activities, except in the cases the OSH Code allows (work usually done through contractors in your industry, work that doesn't need full-time staff, or a sudden rise in work that must be finished in a set time)
- [ ] Standing orders on conditions of service, discipline and misconduct adopted or certified, if an industrial establishment has 300 or more workers (Industrial Relations Code)
- [ ] A crèche, or access to a shared one, if you have 50 or more employees, with women allowed four visits a day (Code on Social Security)
- [ ] With 30 or more workers, apprentices engaged in the band the Apprentices Act, 1961 and its rules set (2.5% to 15% of the workforce, contract workers included), and the contracts registered on the apprenticeship portal

## Policies and grievances

Need a starting point? Adapt the [leave policy template](/toolkit/leave-policy/).

- [ ] Leave policy meets the OSH Code and your state's Shops and Establishments Act
- [ ] Code of conduct and equal opportunity policy written and shared with every employee
- [ ] Grievance Redressal Committee set up if you employ 20 or more workers, with equal employer and worker members and women represented
- [ ] Whistleblower or speak-up policy with a channel that does not go through line managers
- [ ] IT acceptable-use policy, and a remote or hybrid work policy if people work away from the office

## Health and safety

- [ ] Fire safety measures, an evacuation plan and stocked first-aid kits in every office, checked regularly
- [ ] Safety training held at least once a year, including for new joiners
- [ ] Any accident that causes a death, or keeps someone off work for 48 hours or more, reported to the authorities as the OSH Code requires
- [ ] Free annual health check-ups for workers aged 40 and above, and night shifts for women only with their consent and the required safeguards

## Data privacy

The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act's main duties for employers apply from May 2027. Use the time before then to get ready.

- [ ] Employee privacy notice drafted, with the purpose of each use of employee data recorded (most employment uses are allowed without consent as "legitimate uses")
- [ ] Retention periods set for employee and candidate records, with a way to delete what is no longer needed
- [ ] Access to employee data limited to people who need it, and reviewed when roles change
- [ ] Breach response plan written, covering reports to CERT-In within six hours of noticing a covered cyber incident and, from May 2027, notice to the Data Protection Board and affected people

## Exits

- [ ] Final wages paid within two working days of the person leaving, and gratuity within 30 days of it becoming payable
- [ ] Relieving and experience letters issued on or soon after the last working day
- [ ] Exit interviews offered to every leaver, with themes reviewed each quarter
- [ ] Company property recovered against a written list, without holding back wages or letters to force its return
