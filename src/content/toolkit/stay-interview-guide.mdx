Stay interviews ask the questions exit interviews ask, while there is still time to act. The culture questions at the end give you an honest read of how the organisation really works, as opposed to how it describes itself.

## Before the conversation

- **Who:** the person's own manager, after a short briefing from HR on the questions and on how to respond.
- **Who to start with:** people in critical roles and people at known risk points (after a missed promotion, a change of manager, or about 12 to 18 months into a role). Over a year, offer it to everyone, so it isn't seen as a perk for a chosen few.
- **Where and how long:** a private space, 20 to 30 minutes, not tacked onto a performance review or pay discussion.
- **How to open:** explain the purpose ("I want to understand what keeps you here and what I could do better"), and say what you will note down and who will see it.

## The questions

1. What do you look forward to when you come to work?
2. What are you learning here, and what would you like to learn next?
3. What frustrates you that we could fix?
4. Do you feel fairly paid for what you do? Is there anything besides pay that bothers you?
5. When did you last think about leaving, and what prompted it?
6. What would make you want to stay for the next two or three years?
7. What could I do differently as your manager?

<Callout type="tip" title="Listen more than you talk">
Ask, then wait. Follow up with "tell me more" or "what would that look like?" Don't defend, explain or argue during the conversation; there is time to respond later.
</Callout>

- Record themes in a simple tracker: date, main reasons to stay, main frustrations, actions agreed and the follow-up date.
- Share themes with HR, not individual answers. Agree with the person anything you plan to raise with others.
- Every quarter, HR combines themes across teams, without names, and reports the most common reasons to stay and to leave, and what was done about them.
- Check the result: did people whose concerns were addressed stay? Did the same themes come up again?

## Culture diagnostic: five questions

Ask a cross-section of employees these questions anonymously, for example through a short form or in small group sessions run by someone outside their reporting line. Their answers describe the culture people actually experience.

| Question | What the answers tell you |
| --- | --- |
| What kind of person gets promoted here? | Which behaviour is really rewarded |
| What do people get away with here? | What is tolerated, which sets the real standard |
| What happens when someone disagrees with their manager? | Whether it is safe to speak up |
| What does everyone know but no one says out loud? | Unwritten rules and hidden problems |
| If a friend asked whether to join, what would you honestly say? | How people would describe working here when no one is checking |

<Callout type="note" title="Protect people's anonymity">
Report culture answers only as themes, and only when enough people have answered that no one can be identified. Small teams should be combined with others.
</Callout>
