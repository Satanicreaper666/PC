## How the grid works

Each person gets two ratings: **performance** this year (left to right) and **potential** for a bigger role in the next two to three years (bottom to top). The pair puts them in one of nine boxes.

If most people land in one box, check whether managers are using the scales the same way before you act on the result. There is no correct distribution; the point is a fair, evidence-based picture.

## Rating performance and potential

| Level | Performance (this year) | Potential (next two to three years) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Clearly exceeded agreed goals | Ready for a bigger or broader role soon; learns fast and wants to grow |
| Moderate | Met agreed goals | Could grow within the current area, with time and support |
| Low | Missed key goals | Suited to the current scope for now |

If your appraisal uses a 1–5 rating, the paste option can convert it: 1–2 is low, 3 is moderate and 4–5 is high.

<Callout type="warning" title="Use it fairly">
  Potential ratings are especially open to bias. People who work part-time, have recently returned from leave, or are quieter in meetings are easy to under-rate. If one group keeps landing in the lower boxes, look at how the ratings were given before acting on them. An under-performer needs clear expectations and support in writing, and any later decision needs its own fair process; see the [performance improvement plan](/toolkit/performance-improvement-plan/).
</Callout>
