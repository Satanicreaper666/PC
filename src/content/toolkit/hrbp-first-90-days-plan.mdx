The first three months set how leaders and employees see you for years. This plan moves from listening, to fixing what hurts most, to an agreed people plan. Adjust the timing to the size of the business; the order matters more than the exact days.

## Days 1 to 30: listen and learn

The aim is to understand how the business makes money, what leaders worry about and how employees experience HR.

- Meet every leader and business unit head one-to-one
- Hold small-group conversations with employees from every team and level, including new joiners and long-serving staff
- Read the last 12 months of attrition, exit interview themes, engagement results, open grievances and disciplinary cases
- Check compliance basics with the [HR compliance audit checklist](/toolkit/hr-compliance-audit-checklist/), so that no legal gap surprises you later
- Map the HR processes people complain about most: payroll, leave, onboarding, exits
- Write down the five people problems you heard most often, in the words people used

### Questions for your first meetings with leaders

1. What does the business need to achieve this year, and what could stop it?
2. Which roles or teams would hurt most if people left?
3. Where do you feel let down by HR today?
4. What does good look like for you in six months?
5. Who else should I talk to?

## Days 31 to 60: fix what hurts

Use the problems you heard to choose two or three fixes you can deliver within a month. Visible results build the trust you need for bigger changes.

- Fix the most visible pain point first, such as a recurring payroll error, a policy gap or a delayed exit settlement
- Close any legal gaps found in the compliance check, starting with the ones that carry the most risk
- Set up or repair one broken process, such as onboarding, leave approvals or exit settlements
- Build a simple monthly people report, even in a spreadsheet: headcount, hires, exits and attrition by team
- Agree a regular rhythm with each leader: a short monthly meeting with the people report

<Callout type="tip" title="Say what you won't do yet">
Leaders will ask for many things. Write down every request, agree which two or three come first, and tell people when you will come back to the rest. It is better than starting ten things and finishing none.
</Callout>

## Days 61 to 90: plan and agree

- Draft a six-month people plan: three priorities, the measures for each, the budget and who owns what
- Test the draft with two or three leaders before the formal presentation
- Present the plan to the CEO or leadership team and agree what you will report each quarter
- Choose two or three initiatives you will lead personally over the next six months
- Share a short "state of our people" note with leaders: what you heard, what you fixed and what comes next

### What the 90-day people plan should contain

| Section | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| What we heard | The top five people problems, with the evidence behind each |
| What we fixed | Quick wins from days 31 to 60, with before-and-after numbers where you have them |
| Priorities | No more than three, each linked to a business goal |
| Measures | One or two numbers per priority, with today's figure and a target |
| Resources | Budget, people and tools needed, and what you will stop doing |
| Timeline | What happens each month for the next six months |

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">

- Copying the last company's policies before understanding this one
- Promising leaders everything in the first month
- Talking only to leaders and never hearing from employees
- Skipping the compliance check because "nothing has gone wrong yet"

</Callout>
