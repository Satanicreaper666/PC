  - q: What is the difference between the unadjusted and the like-for-like pay gap?
    a: The unadjusted gap compares all women with all men, so it reflects who holds which roles as well as how they are paid. The like-for-like gap compares women and men in the same role, which is closer to the question the law asks about the same or similar work.

    a: The Code on Wages, 2019 says there shall be no discrimination on the ground of gender in wages paid by the same employer for the same work or work of a similar nature. ## How the gaps are worked out

The **unadjusted gap** is the standard headline measure: the difference between men's and women's average pay, as a share of men's.

The **like-for-like gap** compares each woman's pay with the average for men in the same role, then adds it all up.

<Figure title="Like-for-like gap within roles" caption="Only roles with at least one woman and one man count. Roles with more women weigh more, because more women are affected.">

## What to do with a gap

The Code on Wages, 2019 says there shall be no discrimination on the ground of gender in wages for the **same work or work of a similar nature**: work needing the same skill, effort, experience and responsibility under similar conditions. Similar work can sit under different job titles, so look across titles as well as within them.

<Callout type="warning" title="Read small numbers with care">
  This tool marks every role with fewer than 5 women or 5 men. In groups that small, one hire or one exit changes the average, and there are too few people to tell a real pattern from chance. Use those gaps to decide which individual cases to review, not as findings. The tool also doesn't adjust for experience, performance, location or hours.
</Callout>

## Fixing what you find

1. Review the individual cases behind any gap in a role, starting with the largest.
2. Check where differences start: offers, increments or promotions. The [salary band designer](/toolkit/salary-band-designer/) helps you set ranges that apply to everyone.
3. Set aside a small budget for corrections and apply it through the [increment budget allocator](/toolkit/increment-budget-allocator/).
4. Run the analysis again every year, before increments are approved.
