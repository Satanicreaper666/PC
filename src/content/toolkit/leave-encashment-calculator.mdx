## How the amount is worked out

  />
</Figure>

The OSH Code says unused leave is paid as wages, but it does not fix how a day's wages are worked out. Policies usually divide monthly wages by 26 (working days, the same divisor used for gratuity and overtime) or by 30 (calendar days). Dividing by 26 gives a higher figure. Use the wages and divisor your policy names; under the Labour Codes, wages are basic pay, dearness allowance and other pay not on the exclusion list.

A "worker" who works <Fact id="leave.eligibilityDays" /> or more in a calendar year earns annual leave at <Fact id="leave.accrualDays" />. Up to <Fact id="leave.carryForwardMax" /> can be carried forward; leave the worker applied for and was refused can be carried forward without a limit. The PIB note on the Codes puts it simply: every worker can take annual leave after 180 days of work in a year.

"Worker" leaves out people employed mainly in a managerial or administrative role, and supervisors earning more than <Fact id="osh.supervisorWageLimit" />. Their leave comes from the state Shops and Establishments Act and company policy. Whether the Code on Wages' two-working-day rule for final wages also covers their leave encashment is debated, so the safe course is to pay it with the final wages.

<Callout type="tip">
The OSH Code's leave rules apply to establishments with 10 or more workers. State Shops and Establishments Acts continue alongside them, and some allow more carry-forward. Where two rules differ, most employers apply the one that is better for the employee.
</Callout>

## Tax

Income tax on leave encashment depends on why and when it is paid, under the Income-tax Act, 2025. This calculator does not work out tax; see the TDS handbook chapter on [joiners and leavers](/handbooks/tds/joiners-leavers-and-transfers/).
