faq:
  - q: What is a merit matrix?
    a: A grid that sets the salary increase for each combination of performance rating and position in the pay band. Strong performers who are low in their band get the most; people already high in their band get less, because their pay is already ahead.
  - q: How do I make a merit matrix fit the budget?
    a: Work out the weighted average increase, which is each cell's increase weighted by the share of people in it. If it is above the budget, multiply every cell by budget ÷ weighted average. This tool does that and shows the scaled matrix.
  - q: Should people rated below expectations get an increase?
    a: That is a policy choice. Some companies give none, others a small one. Whatever you decide, apply it the same way to everyone and explain it in the revision letter.
  - q: Why does position in the band matter?
    a: Two people with the same rating can be paid very differently. A larger percentage for the person low in the band moves pay towards the midpoint over time and avoids overpaying people already near the maximum.

## How the budget check works

The matrix sets an increase for every combination of rating and position in band. Its cost depends on how many people fall into each cell.

## Shaping the matrix

- **Steepness** is the difference between the top and the middle rows. A steep matrix rewards top performers clearly but leaves less for everyone else.
- **Band position** is the difference between the columns. It decides how quickly pay moves towards the midpoint. Compa-ratio is pay ÷ the midpoint of the person's band; the [salary band designer](/toolkit/salary-band-designer/) works it out.
- **The bottom rows** carry a message. A zero says the rating needs to change before pay does; make sure the appraisal said so first.

## What this tool assumes

<Callout type="note" title="Simplifications">
  <ul>
    <li>Everyone is costed at the average salary (payroll ÷ headcount). If higher earners cluster in some cells, the real cost moves.</li>
    <li>The split by position in band is the same for every rating.</li>
    <li>Increases apply for a full year. A mid-year effective date costs less in the current year and the full amount from the next.</li>
  </ul>
  For exact figures, apply the final matrix to each person's salary in a spreadsheet.
</Callout>
