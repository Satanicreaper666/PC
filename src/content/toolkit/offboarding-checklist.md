faq:
  - q: How soon must the full and final settlement be paid?
    a: The Code on Wages requires wages to be paid within two working days of the employee's resignation, removal, dismissal or retrenchment. It is commonly read as two working days after the last working day, though there is no official clarification yet. Gratuity is due within 30 days of becoming payable.
  - q: Can an employer hold back the final settlement until a laptop is returned?
    a: Holding back wages beyond the legal deadline is risky. Recover property through a clear written list and the deductions your contract and the law allow, and deal with disputes separately from the final pay.
  - q: Does the employer transfer the employee's PF to the new employer?
    a: No. The employer marks the exit date on the EPFO portal; the employee then asks for a transfer or withdrawal through their UAN. HR should tell them how.

## When the decision is confirmed

- [ ] Acknowledge the resignation in writing within two working days, or confirm that the termination letter has been given after a fair process
- [ ] Confirm the last working day in writing, using the notice period in the appointment letter
- [ ] Record any agreed change to the notice period (early release, buy-out or extension) in writing, signed off by the manager and HR
- [ ] Tell payroll, IT, admin and finance the last working day
- [ ] Check whether the person is a 'worker' covered by retrenchment rules, if the exit is a termination for a non-disciplinary reason

## Handover

If you use a written questionnaire alongside the interview, send the [exit interview questionnaire](/toolkit/exit-interview-questionnaire/) in the last week.

- [ ] Agree a handover plan with the manager: open work, deadlines, key contacts and where documents live
- [ ] Move ownership of shared files, mailboxes, code repositories and tools to named colleagues
- [ ] Introduce the successor or the manager to key clients and vendors
- [ ] Book an exit interview for the last week, run by someone other than the person's manager
- [ ] Send the exit questionnaire, if you use one

## Final pay and statutory dues

- [ ] Work out final wages: salary to the last working day, unused earned leave, any notice pay and approved reimbursements
- [ ] Check gratuity eligibility: five years' continuous service in most cases, one year for fixed-term employees, and no minimum on death or disablement
- [ ] Make deductions only where the contract and the law allow them, and show each one on the statement
- [ ] Work out TDS on the final payment, allowing for any tax-exempt part of leave encashment or gratuity
- [ ] Share a written final settlement statement with the employee before payment, and answer their questions

## On the last working day

- [ ] Collect the laptop, phone, ID card, access cards and keys, and give a signed receipt
- [ ] Remove company data from personal devices with the employee present, without opening their personal data
- [ ] Switch off email, systems, VPN and building access at the end of the day
- [ ] Hold the exit interview if it hasn't happened yet
- [ ] Thank them, and tell them who to contact with questions after they leave

## After the exit

Templates: [relieving letter](/toolkit/relieving-letter/) and [experience letter](/toolkit/experience-letter/).

- [ ] Pay final wages within two working days of the last working day
- [ ] Pay gratuity within 30 days of it becoming payable, if they are eligible
- [ ] Issue the relieving letter and experience letter, without waiting for disputes to be resolved
- [ ] Mark the date of exit on the EPFO portal, and tell the employee how to transfer or withdraw their PF using their UAN
- [ ] Update ESI records if they were insured
- [ ] Give them the salary TDS certificate for the year (Form 130 from tax year 2026-27) by 15 June after the year ends
- [ ] Close their personnel file and keep records only as long as your retention policy allows
- [ ] Add exit interview themes to the quarterly review of why people leave
