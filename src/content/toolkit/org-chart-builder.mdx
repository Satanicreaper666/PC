## How it works

The builder reads one row per person and links each person to their manager. It matches managers by employee ID first, then by name, and checks the result before it draws anything.

### What the figures mean

- **Levels** count down from the top of the chart to the deepest person. The head of the organisation is level 1.
- **Managers** are people with at least one direct report. Everyone else is an **individual contributor**, whatever their title.
- **Span of control** is the number of people who report directly to a manager. The tool shows the median and the average, and lists managers outside the range you choose.

A **single point of failure** here is the only person in a department who manages anyone, with at least five of the department's people in their reporting line. You can change the five. **Managers with one direct report** are listed too, because a level with one person under it is often worth a second look.

None of these figures is good or bad on its own. A wide span can work well for similar, well-defined work, and a narrow one can be right for a new team or senior specialists. The tool shows what the structure is so you can ask why.

## How to prepare your file

The tool recognises the usual headings from Indian HR systems and spreadsheets, so you rarely need to rename anything. You can also start from the blank template in the tool.

| Column | Used for | Headings it recognises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name (required) | The name on each box | Name, Employee Name, Full Name |
| Employee ID | Linking people to their manager, finding duplicates | Employee ID, Emp Code, ECode, Staff ID |
| Manager's employee ID | Who each person reports to | Reporting Manager ID, Manager Code, RM Code, Reports To |
| Manager's name | Who each person reports to, if there are no IDs | Reporting Manager Name, Manager Name, Reports To |
| Job title | The line under each name | Designation, Job Title, Position, Role |
| Department | Colours, the department breakdown, single points of failure | Department, Dept, Function, Business Unit |
| Location | The location breakdown | Location, Work Location, Branch, City |

A column headed just "Reporting Manager" or "Reports To" is read as IDs or names depending on what it holds. Values such as "Asha Rao (E102)" or "E102 - Asha Rao" are matched on the ID.

### What the data checks look for

| Check | What the chart does |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting loop, such as A reports to B and B reports to A | Breaks the loop at the person who comes first in the file and shows them at the top |
| Employee ID used more than once | Places reports under the first row with that ID |
| Manager not found in the file | Shows the person at the top of a separate chart |
| Manager's name matches more than one person | Shows the person at the top of a separate chart; use IDs instead |
| More than one person with no manager | Shows each of them at the top, side by side |
| Listed as their own manager | Treats them as having no manager, as many HR systems record the head of the organisation |

## Your data stays on your device

<Callout type="note" title="What happens to your file">
  <ul>
    <li>Your browser reads the file, draws the chart and makes every download on your device. The file is never uploaded to PeopleCraft or anyone else.</li>
    <li>Nothing is stored unless you tick <strong>Keep this list in this browser</strong>. That keeps one copy in this browser's local storage on this device; untick the box or choose <strong>Delete it</strong> to remove it.</li>
    <li>Reload or close the page without ticking it, and the list is gone.</li>
  </ul>

Employee records are personal data under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Its Rules were notified in November 2025, and most of the duties they place on organisations apply after an 18-month phase-in. Working on your own device means the file doesn't pass to another service. Treat the chart and its downloads as confidential all the same: they name people and who they report to.

## Reading the chart well

- **Start with the data checks.** A missing or wrong manager puts someone at the top of a separate chart and changes every figure above them.
- **Compare like with like.** A sales team of 12 field executives and a team of 12 engineers make different demands on a manager. Look at spans within a function before comparing across functions.
- **Look at managers with one report.** Each one adds a level between the work and the decision. Some are deliberate, such as a new team being built; others are left over from a reorganisation.
- **Use it for succession conversations.** A single point of failure is a prompt to ask who could step in, not a verdict. The [stay interview guide](/toolkit/stay-interview-guide/) and [people analytics dashboard](/toolkit/people-analytics/) help with the next questions.
