faq:
  - q: What is a calibration meeting?
    a: Managers and HR review proposed ratings together, compare people across teams against the same standard, and agree changes before ratings are final. It reduces the effect of a generous or strict manager on someone's rating, pay and promotion.
  - q: Should a nine-box use a forced distribution?
    a: No. Guide ranges, such as 5% to 15% in the top-right box, help start the conversation, but forcing people into boxes to hit a curve damages trust and is hard to defend. Use differences from the guide as questions, not quotas.

## What the review checks

- **The grid.** Everyone with both ratings, in one of nine boxes. The [nine-box talent mapper](/toolkit/nine-box-talent-mapper/) explains each box and the suggested action.
- **Distribution.** The share in the top-right and bottom-left boxes against a guide range. A very full top-right box usually means ratings are inflated; an empty bottom row in a large group can mean the same.
- **Calibration by manager.** Each manager's average performance level against the company's. Teams of five or more that sit half a level or more away are flagged. A real difference is possible, so ask for the evidence before changing anything.
- **Fairness.** Women's share of the high-potential row, the high-performance column and the top-right box, against their share of everyone reviewed. A gap of ten points or more is flagged, with average tenure by potential level.
- **Succession and retention.** The people who could step up, with their role, manager and flight risk, and any critical role with no possible successor in its department.

The file is read in your browser and isn't uploaded. Groups smaller than five aren't judged, and nothing is kept after you leave the page.
