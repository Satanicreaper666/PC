The Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 set the employer's share at <Fact id="esi.employerRate" /> and the employee's share at <Fact id="esi.employeeRate" />, each "rounded to the next higher rupee". Wages are worked out as the Code on Social Security defines them: basic pay, dearness allowance and other pay not on the exclusion list, plus any excess of excluded allowances over half of total pay. The Labour Ministry's FAQs say this definition governs ESI contributions from 21 November 2025.

### Contribution periods

ESI runs in two contribution periods: <Fact id="esi.contributionPeriods" />. An employee covered at the start of a period stays covered until it ends, even if a raise takes wages above the limit, and contributions continue on actual wages until then. Coverage is tested again at the start of the next period.

<Callout type="warning" title="Rules in transition">
The Code on Social Security keeps the old ESI rules, regulations and schemes in force for one year from 21 November 2025, that is until <Fact id="esi.transitionEnds" />. New ESI regulations, and any change to the wage limit, had not been published by late October 2026. Check the wage limit and rates again after that date.
</Callout>

### Very low wages

ESIC's rules have exempted employees whose average daily wage is <Fact id="esi.lowWageExemption" /> or less from paying their own share; the employer still pays. This figure comes from secondary sources, so confirm it with ESIC before relying on it.

## What this calculator leaves out

- Benefits: sickness, maternity, disablement and medical care.
- The government's support for employers of employees with disabilities.
- Interest and damages for late payment.
