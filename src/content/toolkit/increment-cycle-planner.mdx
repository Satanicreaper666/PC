faq:
  - q: What is a merit matrix?
    a: A table of increase percentages by performance rating and position in the salary band (compa-ratio). People with higher ratings get more, and people low in their band get more than people with the same rating who are already high in it, so pay moves towards the midpoint over time.
  - q: How should joiners be treated in the increment cycle?
    a: Most companies leave out people who joined in the last three months and prorate the increase for those who joined during the review year, by months served out of twelve. Set both rules here and apply them to everyone the same way.
  - q: Should promotions come out of the merit budget?
    a: It is clearer to keep a separate promotion budget, often 0.5% to 1.5% of payroll. Then merit increases reflect performance, and promotion increases reflect a bigger role.

## How it works

<Figure title="One person's increase" caption="Applied to every row of your file. The merit budget and the promotion budget are checked separately.">
  <Formula
    result={{ value: "New salary" }}
    parts={[{ value: "Salary" }, { op: "×" }, { value: "1 + increase %" }]}
    example="Example: ₹8,00,000, rated 4 out of 5 (exceeds expectations), compa-ratio 0.80 (low in the band): 11% → ₹8,88,000. Joined six months before the effective date: 5.5% → ₹8,44,000."
  />
</Figure>

1. **Compa-ratio** is salary ÷ band midpoint. Below 0.90 is low in the band, 0.90 to 1.10 is around the midpoint, above 1.10 is high.
2. **Matrix %** is the cell for the person's rating and compa-ratio zone.
3. **Proration**: people who joined in the 12 months before the effective date get months served ÷ 12 of the matrix increase. People who joined after the cut-off date are left out.
4. **Fit to budget** (optional) scales every cell by the same factor, so merit spends exactly the budget and the matrix keeps its shape.
5. **Promotion %** is added on top for people marked for promotion, and checked against its own budget.

## Preparing your file

Export one row per person in the review, with a header row. The tool recognises common HRMS column names; the blank template shows the exact ones.

| Column | Needed? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual CTC or fixed pay | Yes | Use the same pay element the increase applies to |
| Final rating | Yes | 1 to 5, or words such as "Exceeds expectations" |
| Band midpoint | Better | Otherwise the grade's median salary is used |
| Band maximum | Optional | Flags anyone whose new salary goes above it |
| Grade, department, manager | Better | For compa-ratio fallback and the totals |
| Date of joining | Better | For proration and the cut-off |
| Promotion, new designation | Optional | "Yes" in the promotion column |

## From the cycle to the letters

**Salary revision letters (CSV for mail merge)** downloads one row per person getting an increase, with the [salary revision letter](/toolkit/salary-revision-letter/)'s own column names. In the [mail merge](/toolkit/mail-merge/), choose that letter and load the file: the company details and signatory come from your saved company details, and every letter is ready to download in one Word file.
