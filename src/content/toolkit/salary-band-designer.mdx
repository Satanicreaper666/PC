    a: How far pay has moved through the range, from 0% at the minimum to 100% at the maximum. It is (pay − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum).

    a: Usually, yes. Overlap lets an experienced person in one grade earn more than a newcomer in the grade above. Too much overlap blurs the grades; a gap turns every promotion into a large jump.

## How the bands are worked out

Each grade has a **midpoint**: the pay for someone fully competent in the role. The minimum and maximum sit either side of it, set by the **range spread**.

<Figure title="Minimum and maximum from the midpoint" caption="Spread is measured from the minimum, so the midpoint sits exactly halfway between minimum and maximum.">
  <Formula
    result={{ value: "Minimum", label: "bottom of the range" }}
    parts={[{ value: "Midpoint", label: "target pay for the grade" }, { op: "÷" }, { value: "1 + spread ÷ 2", label: "e.g. 1 + 0.40 ÷ 2 = 1.20" }]}
    example="Example, grade 1: ₹4,00,000 ÷ 1.20 = ₹3,33,333. Maximum = minimum × (1 + spread) = ₹3,33,333 × 1.40 = ₹4,66,667."
  />
</Figure>

Midpoints then rise by the **progression** from one grade to the next: grade 2's midpoint is ₹4,00,000 × 1.25 = ₹5,00,000 in the example, grade 3's is ₹6,25,000, and so on.

| Measure | Formula | Example (from the calculator's starting values) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Range spread | (maximum − minimum) ÷ minimum | Grade 3: 48% |
| Midpoint progression | (next midpoint − this midpoint) ÷ this midpoint | 25% between every grade |
| Overlap with the next grade | (this maximum − next minimum) ÷ (this maximum − this minimum) | Grade 2 into grade 3: 48% |
| Compa-ratio | pay ÷ midpoint | ₹6,00,000 ÷ ₹6,25,000 = 0.96 |
| Range penetration | (pay − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum) | ₹6,00,000 in grade 3: 40% |

When two neighbouring grades have the same spread, overlap = 1 − progression ÷ spread. A 50% spread with 25% progression gives 50% overlap; raise the progression to 50% and the grades stop overlapping.

## Choosing the numbers

### Midpoints

Start from the market. Match each grade's main roles to a salary survey, take the figure that matches your pay policy (for example the median if you aim to pay at the market) and set the lowest grade's midpoint from it. If the progression doesn't land close to the market for higher grades, adjust the progression rather than forcing every grade.

### Progression

Progression decides how big a promotion feels. Small steps suit a structure with many grades; larger steps suit a flatter organisation with fewer, broader grades.

### Spread

Spread decides how much room people have to grow without a promotion. Widen it for senior grades, where pay depends more on experience and scope.

## Using bands fairly

The Code on Wages, 2019 says there shall be no discrimination on the ground of gender in wages for the **same work or work of a similar nature**, which it defines by the skill, effort, experience and responsibility the work needs, done under similar conditions. Bands help you show that pay follows the work. Run the [pay equity analyser](/toolkit/pay-equity-analyzer/) on each grade once a year to check that it does.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
  <ul>
    <li>Building bands from what you pay today, which locks in past inconsistencies.</li>
    <li>Treating the maximum as a target. Most people who are good at their job should sit around the midpoint.</li>
    <li>Promoting someone only because they reached the top of their range. Promotion should follow a bigger role.</li>
  </ul>
</Callout>
