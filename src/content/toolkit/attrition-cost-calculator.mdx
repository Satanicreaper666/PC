faq:
  - q: How much does it cost to replace an employee?
    a: It depends on the role, how long the seat stays empty and how long a new person takes to learn the job. Published estimates vary widely because they count different things, so this calculator builds the figure from your own assumptions.
  - q: Is attrition cost the same as cost per hire?
    a: No. Cost per hire is only the hiring part. Attrition cost adds the work lost while the seat is empty, onboarding, and the months a new person needs to reach full output.
  - q: Should I count the notice period?
    a: Count only the days the seat is actually empty. If the replacement joins on or before the leaver's last day, the empty-seat cost is zero, though handover time still counts.
  - q: Why use CTC as the value of a day's work?
    a: It is a simple, conservative stand-in for what a day's work is worth to you. For roles that bring in revenue directly, such as sales, the real loss is usually higher, so treat the result as a floor.

| Part | How it is worked out | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty seat | Day's value × working days empty × share of work not covered | ₹4,800 × 30 × 50% = ₹72,000 |
| Hiring | Annual CTC × agency fee % + other hiring costs | ₹99,960 + ₹15,000 = ₹1,14,960 |
| Interview and handover time | Hours × hourly cost of the people involved | 30 × ₹1,000 = ₹30,000 |
| Onboarding and training | Induction, training, equipment and set-up | ₹50,000 |
| Ramp-up | Monthly CTC × months to full output × (100% − output while learning) | ₹1,00,000 × 3 × 40% = ₹1,20,000 |

A day's value is the annual CTC divided by working days in a year: ₹12,00,000 ÷ 250 = ₹4,800.

## Using the number

- **Make a retention case.** If a role costs ₹3.9 lakh to replace and five people leave it each year, a fix that keeps two of them is worth about ₹7.7 lakh a year.
- **Plan the year.** Multiply by the leavers you expect, using the attrition rate from your [headcount plan](/toolkit/headcount-planning-model/).
- **Find the biggest lever.** If ramp-up dominates, better onboarding pays off; if the empty seat dominates, start hiring during the notice period.

<Callout type="note" title="What this leaves out">
  Lost clients, delayed projects, overtime by colleagues and the effect on the team are real costs, but hard to price fairly. Add them only where you can put a number on them, and say so when you present the result. [Stay interviews](/toolkit/stay-interview-guide/) and [exit interviews](/toolkit/exit-interview-questionnaire/) help you find out why people leave in the first place.
</Callout>
