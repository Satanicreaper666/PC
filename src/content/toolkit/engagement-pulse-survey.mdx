## How to use it

If you are answering for yourself, there are no right answers, and your answers stay in this browser.

To run it with a team, there are two ways. Both keep every answer exact, so area scores and bands are right:

- **One device, in a meeting.** Each person answers, then presses **Add another person's answers**. The form clears for the next person and the team's averages build up below, hidden until you open them.
- **An anonymous form tool.** Copy the twelve statements into Google Forms or Microsoft Forms with the same 1 to 5 scale, collect the answers, download them as CSV and use **Import responses (CSV)** above. Matching columns are found by the statement's wording. Area scores, the share of people agreeing and cuts by team (only for groups of five or more) are worked out here; nothing is uploaded.

Don't enter rounded team averages as if they were one person's answers: rounding can move an area by up to half a point and change its band.

## Reading the results

- Start with the lowest area, not the overall score. One fix that people notice does more than a long action plan.
- Two statements are worded the other way round, about hesitating to speak up and thinking about leaving. The score flips them, so a higher score is always better.
- Watch the trend. A drop of half a point in an area between quarters is worth a conversation, even if the score still looks healthy.

<Callout type="warning" title="Protect anonymity">
Report results only for groups of five or more, and never try to work out who said what. Once people suspect their answers can be traced, the scores stop meaning anything.
</Callout>

## Adding an eNPS question

Many organisations add one more question in their own survey tool: "On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend this organisation as a place to work?" Keep it separate from the twelve statements, because it uses a different scale.
