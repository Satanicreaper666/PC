## Read the exam before you read the books

The Chennai HR executive's two scores tell her more than any study guide could. Her knowledge is in good shape. Her judgement is not, and judgement items are {CRED.shrmMix.value[0]}% of the exam. "More questions" is advice about volume; what she needs first is a picture of how the exam is built and scored, so her eight weeks go where the marks are.

Both SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP follow the same format, set out in SHRM's 2026 Certification Handbook:

- **{CRED.shrmItems.display}, all multiple choice.** {CRED.shrmScored.display}; the other 24 are field-test questions mixed in at random. You can't tell which is which, so treat every question as live.
- **Three item types.** Situational judgement items (SJIs) test the behavioural competencies through workplace scenarios. Foundational knowledge items test the concepts behind those competencies, such as change models or basic statistics. HR-specific knowledge items test the 14 functional areas. The split is {CRED.shrmMix.display}.
- **Fixed pass mark.** Your raw score becomes a scaled score from 120 to 200, and {CRED.shrmPassMark.display} is a pass. There is no curve: a panel sets the standard in advance, using a modified Angoff method, so you are not competing with the people beside you.
- **No partial credit.** On SJIs, only the most effective response earns the point.

## What the 2026 BASK tests, and how much

Every SHRM question is written against one blueprint, the SHRM BASK (Body of Applied Skills and Knowledge), 2026 edition. Half the exam covers eight **behavioural competencies** in three clusters; the other half covers **HR expertise**: three knowledge domains made up of 14 functional areas. The weights are the same for CP and SCP.

**What changed in 2026.**

- **Nine competencies became eight.** Inclusive Mindset replaced Inclusion & Diversity and Global Mindset, and now sits in the Leadership cluster.
- **AI appears throughout,** especially in HR expertise: AI-assisted screening, generative AI in learning, AI policies and awareness of bias in data.
- **Weight moved** towards Leadership, Business and People, and away from Interpersonal and Workplace.

Some SHRM web pages still used older wording during 2026, so revise from the 2026 BASK and Handbook.

**How the two halves are tested.** Behavioural competencies are scored mostly through SJIs, plus the foundational knowledge items. Functional areas are scored through knowledge items, although SJI scenarios draw on them: a question about Ethical Practice may be set in a pay dispute or a restructuring. So your knowledge of HR functions shows up twice, once directly and once as the setting for judgement.

## CP or SCP: one scenario at two altitudes

SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP use the same blueprint, the same weights, the same mix of item types and the same length. Three things differ:

1. **The standard.** CP is pitched at operational HR work; SCP at strategic work, drawing on the BASK's indicators "for advanced HR professionals".
2. **The altitude of the scenarios.** CP stems usually cast you as a generalist or HR business partner dealing with a manager or an employee. SCP stems usually make you an HR director or CHRO facing a decision for the whole organisation.
3. **The pass mark.** Each exam's standard is set against a borderline candidate at its own level, operational for CP and strategic for SCP.

The difference shows in the pass rates: {CRED.shrmPassCp.display} for SHRM-CP and {CRED.shrmPassScp.display} for SHRM-SCP in the May–July 2026 window, and {CRED.shrmPassRange.display} across the seven windows from May 2023. To sit SCP you need {CRED.shrmScpYears.display} of strategic-level HR work, or three years holding CP.

In SCP scenarios the best answer usually gathers evidence, frames the business case, builds agreement among executives and sets a phased plan with measures. A purely tactical fix that you carry out yourself is usually a distractor, however competent it sounds. The reverse trap catches CP candidates who over-study SCP material: on a CP item, "build a business case with the CEO" is often the wrong altitude when the best answer is to coach the manager.

## How judgement items are scored, and how to pick the best response

SHRM builds each situational judgement item from a real situation that SHRM-certified professionals have faced. Each item draws on {CRED.shrmSjiWriters.display} HR professionals, who write, review and validate it. Each scenario carries two or three questions, and each question probes a different part of the situation.

The scoring is what most candidates underestimate:

- **Every option is plausible.** No answer is obviously silly. A panel of certified practitioners agrees which response is most effective, judged against the BASK's indicators.
- **Only the best response scores.** The other options range from ineffective to marginally effective, and an acceptable option scores the same as a poor one.
- **There is no "SHRM answer" to memorise.** SHRM staff don't write the items. Answer from good practice as the BASK describes it, not from how your own company does things, and don't add facts the stem doesn't give you.

<Worked title="Worked example: one judgement item, four options">

*This is an original practice item, not a SHRM item.*

> You are the HR business partner for a 700-person customer-service centre in Noida. Three agents separately report that a team leader shouts at them and mocks their mistakes in team huddles. His team has the best call-quality scores in the centre. The operations head says: "He gets results. Let's not lose him over his tone." What is the most effective response?
>
> **A.** Agree with the operations head and keep an informal eye on the huddles for a month.
>
> **B.** Suspend the team leader while the complaints are investigated.
>
> **C.** Tell the operations head the reports must be looked into; plan a prompt, impartial fact-finding; protect the three agents from retaliation; and agree interim steps, such as a second leader attending the huddles.
>
> **D.** Enrol the team leader in a communication-skills workshop.

**Step 1: role, level, question.** You are an HR business partner, so this is CP altitude. The question asks for the most effective response overall, not the first step.

**Step 2: competency.** Three separate reports of humiliation engage Ethical Practice (impartial investigation, protection from retaliation) and Relationship Management (handling conflict at its source). Results don't cancel conduct.

**Step 3: strike out the extremes.**

| Option | What it does | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Lets results excuse conduct; the agents are left exposed and learn that reporting doesn't work | Ineffective |
| B | Acts, but disciplines before any facts are established; out of proportion at this stage | Marginally effective |
| D | Well meant, but treats a conduct complaint as a training need without finding out what happened | Marginally effective |
| C | Gathers facts fairly, protects the reporters, manages risk now and keeps the business informed | **Best** |

**Step 4: root cause, fair process, right people.** Only C establishes the facts before deciding, documents a fair process, and involves the operations head without letting him decide the outcome.

**Step 5.** B and D each do part of the job. C is the complete answer for a CP-level HR business partner.

**Key: C.** If you chose B, notice the pattern: decisive action feels strong, but SHRM keys usually reward fact-finding before discipline unless someone is at immediate risk.

</Worked>

## An eight-week sprint to exam day

A sprint assumes you have already covered the content, through this course or otherwise. Its job is to turn knowledge into exam performance. Four rules from the research on learning shape it:

- **Test, don't reread.** Timed question sets are the core of every week. Rereading feels productive but builds familiarity, not recall.
- **Space it.** Come back to each area every week or two, rather than finishing it and moving on.
- **Mix it.** The exam mixes every area, so your practice sets should too, once the weakest areas are rebuilt.
- **Learn from every error.** Keep an error log. For each miss, note the competency, the option you chose, the keyed answer and why it beat yours. Most people find two or three repeating patterns, such as acting before gathering facts or doing the task themselves instead of coaching the manager.

**A week-by-week plan (example, at about 6–8 hours a week).**

| Week | Focus | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find where you lose marks | One timed, mixed set of about 60 questions; sort every error by competency and by type of mistake |
| 2 | Highest priority: biggest weight × weakness | Daily short sets in that area; read the feedback on every option, not just the key |
| 3 | Second priority | As week 2, plus ten minutes on week 2's errors |
| 4 | Third priority | As week 3; start mixing in questions from weeks 2–3 |
| 5 | Exam conditions | Two timed mixed sets of about 50 questions each, at exam pace |
| 6 | Full-length mock | All {CRED.shrmItems.value} questions in two timed sections; review every wrong and every guessed answer |
| 7 | Close the gaps the mock showed | Re-test old errors without looking; short mixed sets |
| 8 | Taper | A light set early in the week; cheat sheets; confirm the centre, route, ID and timing |

**Where to find questions.** SHRM's own Certification Prep System includes practice questions and a full-length practice exam built from items used on earlier exams, and SHRM also sells half-length practice tests. This course's quizzes give you more judgement practice, built on Indian situations. Use third-party question banks with care: some are keyed to a different logic from SHRM's.

For the Chennai candidate, the plan shifts towards judgement: about two-thirds of her practice time on SJIs, an error log by competency, and the five-step method on every item, with knowledge sets mixed in to keep her strong areas warm.

## Where experts disagree

Expect these points in a CIPD Level 7 discussion of assessment, or an XLRI class on selection.

- **What judgement items measure.** Situational judgement tests predict job performance moderately well, and their results overlap substantially with general mental ability (McDaniel et al., 2001). Researchers still debate whether they measure judgement, job knowledge or general ability, and how much each contributes.
- **Whose "best" answer?** The key reflects one expert panel's consensus. Critics argue that no single key fits every culture and organisation: what works in a US firm may differ in a hierarchical Indian family business. SHRM's reply is that items test practice as the BASK defines it, not local custom.
- **Knowing versus doing.** Choosing the right option on screen is not the same as acting on it in a tense meeting, or sustaining it for months. A certification is evidence of knowledge and judgement, not proof of performance.
- **Credentials and results.** Most research examines the tests, not whether holding the credential leads to better HR outcomes. Employers who value a credential are partly buying a signal of commitment and a shared vocabulary.

A good answer acknowledges what the method does well, names its limits with evidence, and argues for combining it with other evidence of competence.
