## Three syllabi, one profession

XLRI teaches HR inside a management degree. CIPD teaches it as a profession, with published standards and a code of conduct. SHRM describes it as a set of competencies and tests them in an exam. Underneath, all three describe the same job: helping an organisation get, grow, pay and keep the people it needs, fairly and within the law.

This course was built by laying the three syllabi side by side: XLRI's PGDM (HRM) prospectus for 2027, CIPD's 2026 Level 5 and Level 7 specifications, and SHRM's 2026 BASK. Wherever two or three teach the same idea, you learn it once, in one module, then practise it the way each of them would test it.

## What all three share, and what only one teaches

The overlap is large. Hiring, development, performance, reward, employee relations, ethics, change and strategy appear in every syllabus, under different names. What differs is the law each one teaches, how much business it covers, and how it makes you prove what you know.

Two frameworks sit behind the shared centre. CIPD's **Profession Map** sets out 6 areas of core knowledge, 8 core behaviours and 11 specialist areas, each at four career levels. SHRM's **BASK** has 8 behavioural competencies and 14 functional areas, each with indicators for all HR professionals and for advanced ones. Their behaviours line up closely: ethical practice, courage and influence, working inclusively, business sense and evidence appear in both. XLRI has no published competency framework; its course list does the same job.

## Where they disagree

The points where the three part company are the most useful to understand. They are where exam questions and assignment criteria get difficult, and where a thoughtful answer stands out.

What this means when you are tested:

- **Unions.** In a SHRM judgement item, the most effective answer is rarely anti-union: it respects the process and the law. In a CIPD Level 7 essay you must compare the unitarist, pluralist and radical frames, not just pick one. India's Industrial Relations Code writes pluralism into law through the negotiating union or council.
- **Evidence.** CIPD wants your sources and your reasoning on the page. SHRM wants you to choose the option that gathers evidence before acting. XLRI wants you to defend your analysis of a case in front of the class.
- **Inclusion.** Whatever the label, in India the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, the Transgender Persons Act and the POSH Act are legal duties, not optional programmes.
- **Law.** Each body tests a different statute book, but the same principles travel across all three: natural justice and due process, documentation, consistency, non-discrimination, proportionality, and consultation before change. When you are unsure of a rule, reason from these.
- **Business.** CIPD Level 7 and SHRM-SCP take business literacy for granted. [Module 1](/learn/business-acumen-for-hr/) teaches it the XLRI way, as a subject in its own right.

This course pitches Blocks A to C at SHRM-CP and CIPD Level 5, then stretches to SHRM-SCP and Level 7 in Blocks D to F. Strategic-level material is marked in every module.

## What none of them covers for India

Some of what an Indian HR leader needs in 2026 is missing from all three syllabi, or covered only lightly. These are the topics this course adds.

| Gap | Why it matters | Where it is taught here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The DPDP Act and Rules for employee data | The main duties apply from about 13 May 2027 and reach every HR record, from applications to exit files | [5](/learn/talent-acquisition/), [11](/learn/indian-labour-law/), [17](/learn/hr-technology-and-ai/) |
| The Labour Codes transition, state by state | The four Codes have been in force since 21 Nov 2025, but Karnataka, Goa and Delhi rules were still in draft in November 2026, so employers work under a mix of old and new rules | [11](/learn/indian-labour-law/) |
| Gig and platform workers | The Code on Social Security and Karnataka's 2025 law on platform-based gig workers create new duties for employers and aggregators | [11](/learn/indian-labour-law/), [17](/learn/hr-technology-and-ai/) |
| BRSR Core people metrics | SEBI requires large listed companies to report core people metrics, such as wages paid to women and POSH complaints, with assessment or assurance phased in by company size | [3](/learn/evidence-based-hr/), [18](/learn/global-hr-and-esg/) |
| HR for global capability centres (GCCs) | Global job architecture, and exposure to the EU AI Act and EU pay-transparency rules through the parent company | [8](/learn/reward-and-total-rewards/), [17](/learn/hr-technology-and-ai/), [18](/learn/global-hr-and-esg/) |
