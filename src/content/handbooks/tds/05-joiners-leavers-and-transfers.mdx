## New joiners in the middle of the year

TDS is worked out on the employee's estimated salary for the whole tax year. If someone joins in November, their salary from April to October with another employer is part of that year. Section 392(4) lets the employee give you those details, and if they do, you must take them into account.

The employee does this on Form 122, which replaced Forms 12B and 12BAA. It covers salary from other employers in the same tax year (including perquisites and the tax they deducted), loss from house property, other income and any tax deducted or collected on it. It is optional for the employee, so explain why it helps them.

### What to collect on day one

- The regime they intend to use this year. No answer means the new regime.
- Form 122, with the previous employer's salary and TDS for this tax year. Ask for a payslip or a provisional statement from the previous employer if they have one.
- Permanent account number (PAN), checked against the name in your records.
- Form 124 claims, if they are on the old regime.

## Leavers: TDS in the final settlement

When an employee resigns or is asked to leave, the [full and final (F&F) settlement](/glossary/#full-and-final-settlement) usually includes the last month's salary and several one-off items. Recompute the year's tax on what the employee actually earned with you, not on the 12-month projection you used while they were working.

### How common F&F items are taxed

| Item in the settlement | Tax treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Salary for days worked, bonus, incentives | Taxable salary |
| Notice pay paid by the employer | Taxable salary |
| Leave encashment | Non-government employees: exempt up to the limits in section 19 of the 2025 Act; the rest is taxable. Check the current notified limit |
| Gratuity | Exempt up to the limits in section 19; the rest is taxable. Check the current limit with your tax adviser |
| Compensation under an approved voluntary retirement scheme | Exempt up to ₹5,00,000 under section 19 |
| Recovery for notice not served | Reduces what is paid; take advice before reducing taxable salary for it |

### When the employee owes more, not less

A shortfall in the final settlement happens when the last payments are large: a bonus or incentive, arrears from a late increment, or leave encashment above the exempt limit. Deduct the shortfall from the settlement, and tell the employee before the settlement is paid. A surprise deduction at exit damages trust more than the amount justifies.

### Timing

Under the Code on Wages, wages due to an employee who resigns, is removed, dismissed or retrenched must be paid within two working days. Commentators read this as two working days from the last working day, but no official clarification was found. Have the TDS recomputation ready before the last day so the settlement isn't delayed. The [full and final settlement calculator](/toolkit/full-and-final-settlement-calculator/) and [chapter 6 of the labour law handbook](/handbooks/labour-law/termination-grievance-and-disputes/) cover the rest of the settlement.

### Form 130 for leavers

Leavers get Form 130 for their period with you by 15 June after the tax year, like every other employee. When someone had more than one employer in a year, each issues Parts A and B for its own period. Part C, the salary computation, can come from each employer or only from the last one, at the employee's option.

## Transfers within a group

A move to another company in the group is a change of employer for TDS, even if nothing else changes for the employee. Treat it as an exit from one company and a joining at the other: the new company needs Form 122 (with the employee's consent to share the details), and each company issues Form 130 for its period.

A move between branches of the same company is not a change of employer. If the branches deduct TDS under different tax deduction account numbers (TANs), agree which TAN reports the employee for each period so the TDS isn't missed or doubled.

## Employees moving abroad or working from abroad

Whether salary is taxable in India depends on the employee's residential status for the tax year and where the work is done. Salary for work done in India is generally taxable here, even if it is paid abroad. TDS on that salary stays under section 392, and salary paid in foreign currency is converted to rupees at the prescribed rate. Non-residents don't get the section 156 rebate.

Tax treaties and social security agreements can change the answer, and residential status is often unclear in the year someone moves. Collect the facts (travel dates, where the work is done, any tax residency certificate from the other country) and get a tax adviser's view before the first payroll.

## Provident fund withdrawals by leavers

TDS on provident fund (PF) withdrawals is deducted by the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), not by payroll. Under section 392(7), the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) Scheme deducts 10% when a taxable withdrawal is ₹50,000 or more. Remind leavers to keep their PAN updated with the EPFO, and that transferring their PF to the next employer usually avoids both the tax and the TDS that an early withdrawal can trigger.
