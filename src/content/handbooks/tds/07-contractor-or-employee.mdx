## Why classification matters

The same monthly payment is taxed and reported very differently depending on whether the person is your employee or an independent contractor. For an employee you deduct tax at slab rates, file Form 138 and issue Form 130, and the person is also covered by PF, ESI, gratuity and the Labour Codes. For a contractor you deduct a flat percentage, file Form 140 and issue Form 131, and none of the employee benefits apply.

<Callout type="law" title="Manpower supply from 1 April 2026">
The Finance Act, 2026 brought "supply of manpower" within the definition of "work" for TDS. Payments to staffing and manpower agencies therefore carry TDS at the contract rates, not the professional-fee rate that some businesses applied. This settles a long-running doubt about which rate applied.
</Callout>

Indian courts look at the whole relationship, not at one factor or at the title of the contract. The questions below are the ones that matter most in practice. If most answers point to employment, treat the person as an employee or change how the engagement works.

### Red flags in day-to-day practice

| Points to employment | Points to a genuine contract |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed hours, attendance tracking and leave approval | Works to agreed deliverables and deadlines |
| A manager assigns daily tasks | Decides their own methods |
| Company laptop, email ID and business cards | Uses their own tools and identity |
| Same fixed amount every month, whatever is delivered | Invoices per project or milestone |
| Works only for you, for years | Has other clients and markets their services |
| Appears in the org chart and team meetings as staff | Is managed through a contract owner |

## What happens if you get it wrong

If someone you paid as a contractor turns out to be an employee, the payments were salary. TDS should have been worked out at slab rates under section 392, so the difference is a shortfall. Under section 398 the employer is treated as an assessee in default for that shortfall and pays interest at 1% a month or part of a month. A penalty of up to the tax not deducted can also apply; check the penalty section in the Income-tax Act, 2025 before relying on the number.

Section 398 also gives some protection: if the person has filed their return, included the income and paid the tax, and an accountant certifies this, the employer isn't treated as in default for that tax. Interest still runs until the date the person filed their return.

The bigger costs are usually outside income tax: back PF and ESI contributions, gratuity and leave, and the protections the Labour Codes give employees. The labour law handbook covers these in [contracts and fixed-term employment](/handbooks/labour-law/contracts-and-fixed-term-employment/).

<Callout type="tip">
If you need someone full-time for a defined period, a fixed-term employment contract is often cleaner than a long consultancy. Under the Industrial Relations Code, fixed-term employees get at least the same wages and benefits as comparable permanent staff, in proportion to their service, and gratuity after one year. Their salary TDS works like anyone else's.
</Callout>
