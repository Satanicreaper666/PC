## What salary TDS is and why HR owns it

[Tax deducted at source (TDS)](/glossary/#tds) on salary is income tax that the employer collects from pay on the employee's behalf and sends to the government. Section 392 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 tells the employer to deduct it when salary is paid, at the average rate of tax on the employee's estimated salary income for the year.

In most companies HR or payroll does the calculation, because it depends on things only HR knows: the salary structure, joining and leaving dates, the tax regime each employee chooses and the claims they declare. Finance usually deposits the money and files the returns. If either side slips, the company pays interest and penalties, and the employee may face a tax demand later.

### What HR is responsible for

| Task | When | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ask each employee which regime they intend to use | April, and on joining | A written choice on file; no answer means the new regime |
| Collect claims and proofs on Form 124 | Declarations in April; proofs by the company's deadline | Old-regime claims backed by evidence before the last quarter |
| Collect Form 122 from employees with another employer that year | On joining, or when the employee offers it | Previous salary and TDS included in your calculation |
| Compute and deduct TDS | Every payroll | Recomputed after raises, bonuses, arrears and exits |
| Give the perquisite statement (Form 123) | With Form 130 | Values match the 2026 Rules |
| Hand data to Finance | Monthly and quarterly | PANs checked; salary annexure ready for the last quarter |
| Issue Form 130 | By 15 June after the tax year | Downloaded from TRACES, checked and sent to every employee, including leavers |
| Answer employee questions | All year | Clear explanation of the payslip and the year-end certificate |

## What changed on 1 April 2026

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026, and the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (notified on 20 March 2026) replaced the 1962 Rules. The government's stated aim was a simpler law without changing tax policy, and Budget 2026-27 left personal tax rates and slabs where they were. What did change is the vocabulary: section numbers, form numbers and the idea of a "tax year".

<Callout type="law" title="Which law applies to which year">
Salary paid in tax year 2026-27 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027) is taxed under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Salary for the year ended 31 March 2026 and earlier stays under the 1961 Act, so the Form 16 that employers issued by 15 June 2026 still used the old law.
</Callout>

## The two tax regimes in one view

Under section 202 of the 2025 Act, the new tax regime applies to every individual unless they choose otherwise. A salaried employee with no business income makes the final choice when they file their return for the year. For TDS, the employer needs to know the employee's intention at the start of the year. If the employee says nothing, deduct under the new regime.

| Feature | New regime (default) | Old regime (employee must opt for it) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| No tax for residents if taxable income is up to | ₹12 lakh (rebate of up to ₹60,000) | ₹5 lakh (rebate of up to ₹12,500) |
| House rent allowance (HRA) and leave travel allowance (LTA) exemptions | Not available | Available, with proofs |
| Investment and insurance deductions (old sections 80C, 80D) | Not available | Available, with proofs |
| Professional tax deduction | Not available | Available |
| Employer's National Pension System (NPS) contribution | Deductible up to 14% of basic pay + dearness allowance (DA) | Deductible up to 10% of basic + DA (14% for government employers) |
| Best for | Most employees, and anyone with few deductions | Employees with large HRA, home-loan and investment claims |

[Chapter 2](/handbooks/tds/salary-tds/) shows the slabs, the calculation and when the old regime can still save tax.
