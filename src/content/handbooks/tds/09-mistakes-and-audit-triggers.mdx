## The ten mistakes payroll makes most

| Mistake | What goes wrong | How to prevent it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not asking joiners for Form 122 | Each employer taxes a part-year salary alone; the employee faces a bill at filing | Ask every joiner who had an earlier employer this year, and explain why it helps them |
| Applying the old regime by default | Wrong TDS for anyone who didn't ask for it | The new regime is the default under section 202; use the old regime only on the employee's request |
| Allowing HRA without the landlord's PAN | Claims don't meet rule 205 when the year's rent is above ₹1 lakh | Hold the exemption until the name, address, PAN and any relationship are given |
| Using 1962 perquisite values | Car, gift, meal and loan values are too low, so TDS falls short | Update payroll to rule 15 of the 2026 Rules |
| Projecting a full year for a leaver | Wrong TDS in the final settlement | Recompute on actual earnings with you plus the full and final (F&F) settlement items |
| Deducting TDS within the ₹12 lakh rebate | Residents lose take-home pay they are entitled to all year | Apply the section 156 rebate and marginal relief in the projection |
| Wrong or missing PAN | Credit goes to the wrong person or nobody; higher TDS rates can apply | Validate PANs at joining and before each quarterly statement |
| Late Form 138 or Form 130 | Late fees, and certificates that arrive after employees need them | File the last quarter's statement by mid-May |
| Arrears paid without mentioning relief | The employee pays more tax than necessary | Tell them about section 157 relief and take it into account if they ask |
| Declarations with no proofs | A large deduction in March | Set a proof deadline in January or February and recompute straight after |

## How problems come to light

The department doesn't need an audit to find most TDS errors. They show up in three places:

- **TRACES**, after the Centralised Processing Cell (CPC-TDS) processes each quarterly statement. It reports short deduction, short payment, interest on late deduction or payment, late filing fees and PAN errors as defaults, with a justification report for each.
- **Employees' tax returns**, which are matched with the TDS credited against their PAN and the salary in their Annual Information Statement (AIS). A mismatch becomes a question to the employee, who then comes to you.
- **Information from other sources** shown in AIS, such as rent, interest and investments, which can contradict claims allowed in payroll.

## Myths and facts

| What people say | What is actually true |
| --- | --- |
| "Employees can claim HRA in the new regime." | No. HRA is fully taxable in the new regime. |
| "Every joiner must give Form 122." | It is optional for the employee, but ask every joiner who had another employer earlier in the tax year. |
| "Only a house property loss can reduce salary TDS." | True for the old regime, under section 392(4)(b). In the new regime not even that loss can be set against salary. |
| "Nobody earning up to ₹12 lakh pays tax." | ₹12 lakh is the limit for taxable income for residents in the new regime; with the standard deduction that is about ₹12.75 lakh of taxable salary. Non-residents don't get the rebate. |
| "Section 392 is just section 192 renumbered." | The method is the same, but forms, section numbers, perquisite values and the HRA city list all changed with the 2025 Act and the 2026 Rules. |
