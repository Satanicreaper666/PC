## The yearly declaration cycle

Section 392(5)(b) requires the employer to collect evidence for the claims it allows when working out tax deducted at source (TDS). Rule 205 says the employee gives this on Form 124. The law doesn't set dates for the cycle within the year, so the dates below are common practice; set your own and tell employees early.

### What employees declare in April

- **Their regime for TDS.** If they say nothing, deduct under the new regime, which is the default under section 202. The employee makes the final choice in their tax return, so a different choice there changes only their refund or the tax they pay at filing.
- **Old-regime claims on Form 124**, with the amounts they expect to spend or invest during the year.
- **Form 122**, if they had another employer earlier in the year, or other income or tax deducted elsewhere that they want included. It is optional for the employee and has no deadline.

<Callout type="warning" title="There is no mid-year regime window">
Some payroll policies promise a "regime change window" in October or November. The Act has no such window for employees. Many employers fix the regime for TDS once declared, following long-standing CBDT practice, while the employee keeps the right to choose in their return. Check whether CBDT has issued a salary TDS circular for 2026-27 and write your policy to match it.
</Callout>

## What Form 124 asks for

Form 124 is the employee's statement of claims under section 392(5)(b). It goes to the employer, on paper or electronically, and is not uploaded to the income-tax portal. If an employee doesn't submit it, the employer deducts TDS without any claimed exemptions or deductions.

| Claim (old regime only) | What rule 205 requires | Proof to keep on file |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House rent allowance (HRA) | Landlord's name, address and PAN if the year's rent is more than ₹1,00,000; relationship with the landlord, if any | Rent receipts or rent agreement; bank-transfer records help |
| Leave travel concession or allowance (LTC or LTA) | Evidence of the travel expenditure | Tickets, boarding passes, invoices |
| Interest on a home loan | Lender's name, address and PAN | Lender's certificate splitting interest and principal |
| Deductions under Chapter VIII (old sections 80C, 80D and similar) | Evidence of the investment or expenditure | Statements, premium receipts, fee receipts dated within the tax year |

Employees on the new regime don't need to submit Form 124 for these. The standard deduction needs no declaration at all.

## HRA: the calculation and the checks

Under rule 279, the exempt part of HRA is the least of three amounts for the period the employee rented the home. "Salary" here means basic pay plus dearness allowance (DA) if the terms of employment count DA for this purpose; other allowances and perquisites are left out.

The 50% limit now applies in Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. Everywhere else it is 40%. Update your payroll settings: an employee in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune or Ahmedabad may get a larger exemption from April 2026.

### Rent paid to relatives

The rules don't bar an employee from paying rent to a parent or other relative, but Form 124 now asks for the relationship, and such claims draw scrutiny. Ask for evidence that rent is actually paid, such as monthly bank transfers. Remind the employee that the relative should show the rent as income in their own return. Where a claim looks artificial, for example rent paid to someone who lives in the same home and owns it jointly with the employee, ask for more evidence before allowing it.

<Callout type="note">
The income-tax department receives rent and income information from many sources and shows it in each person's Annual Information Statement (AIS). Claims that don't match are more likely to be questioned after the employee files their return, even if payroll allowed them.
</Callout>

## The year-end true-up

Once proofs are in, recompute every old-regime employee's tax for the year using only the claims you accepted. Spread any shortfall over the months left rather than taking it all in March, which can wipe out an employee's March pay. Section 392(5)(c) lets you increase or reduce later deductions in the same tax year to correct earlier ones.

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Proofs match the declaration | No change to TDS |
| Proofs are lower than declared | Increase TDS for the remaining months; explain the change on the payslip |
| Proofs are higher than declared | Reduce TDS for the remaining months |
| No proofs by the deadline | Remove the claims and recompute; tell the employee they can still claim in their return |
| Employee joined mid-year | Run the same cycle from their joining date, with Form 122 for any earlier salary |
