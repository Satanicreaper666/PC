## From CTC to taxable salary

[Cost to company (CTC)](/glossary/#ctc) is what the employer spends on an employee in a year. Taxable salary is narrower. Some parts of CTC are never paid out monthly, and some are paid but not taxed. Getting this difference right is the first step to correct TDS, and to honest offer letters.

In this example the taxable salary is ₹11,40,000 a year. Under the new regime, after the ₹75,000 standard deduction, taxable income is ₹10,65,000, which is within the ₹12 lakh rebate, so TDS is nil even though the CTC is ₹12 lakh.

### What isn't taxed each month

- **Employer PF, National Pension System (NPS) and superannuation contributions.** They become taxable only if together they exceed ₹7.5 lakh in a tax year (section 17(1)(h) of the 2025 Act). The employer's NPS contribution is also deductible under section 124, up to 14% of basic pay plus dearness allowance (DA) in the new regime, and 10% in the old regime for non-government employers.
- **A gratuity provision.** It isn't paid until the employee leaves. When it is paid, it is taxable salary, with an exemption up to the limits in section 19. Check the current exemption limit with your tax adviser before quoting a figure.
- **Genuine reimbursements of official expenses**, supported by bills and a written policy. Reimbursing personal expenses is taxable salary.

<Callout type="note">
The Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) wage ceiling rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026. Employers who contribute only on the ceiling now pay up to ₹3,000 a month each from employer and employee. See the [EPF calculator](/toolkit/epf-calculator/) and [chapter 4 of the labour law handbook](/handbooks/labour-law/social-security-and-benefits/).
</Callout>

## The Labour Codes and salary structure

From 21 November 2025, the Code on Wages defines "wages" as all pay, allowances and other remuneration, minus a fixed list of exclusions such as HRA, conveyance, overtime, commission and the employer's PF contribution. If those excluded items add up to more than half of total remuneration, the excess is added back into wages. A generic "special allowance" is not on the exclusion list, so it already counts as wages.

The rule does not say basic pay must be 50% of CTC. It changes the base on which PF, gratuity and some other benefits are worked out. The labour law handbook explains it fully in [wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/).

### What this does to TDS

| If more of the same CTC becomes "wages" | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Income tax under the new regime | No change from the split itself: basic, HRA and special allowance are all taxable |
| PF contributions | Higher if PF is paid on full wages; no change if paid only on the ₹25,000 ceiling |
| Gratuity | Higher, because gratuity is a multiple of wages |
| Taxable salary within a fixed CTC | Slightly lower if more CTC goes into employer PF, which isn't taxed |
| HRA exemption under the old regime | Can rise or fall: it depends on rent, HRA and basic + DA |
| Take-home pay | Lower if the employee's own PF contribution rises |

<Callout type="warning">
"Higher basic means higher TDS" is a common claim and it is wrong for the new regime. Explain restructuring to employees in terms of take-home pay, PF and gratuity, not tax. The [salary band designer](/toolkit/salary-band-designer/) helps you build structures that pass the 50% test.
</Callout>

## Perquisites: what's taxable and at what value

A perquisite is a benefit in kind, such as a car, a house or a loan. Its taxable value is added to salary for TDS, using the values in rule 15 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Several values went up sharply from 1 April 2026, which matters for both TDS and the cost of benefits.

### How each common perquisite is valued in 2026-27

| Perquisite | Taxable value under the 2026 Rules | What HR should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Car with personal use, employer pays running costs | ₹5,000 a month (up to 1.6 litres or electric) or ₹7,000 (larger), plus ₹3,000 for a chauffeur | Add to salary every month |
| Car with personal use, employee pays personal running costs | ₹2,000 or ₹3,000 a month, plus ₹3,000 for a chauffeur | Keep evidence of who pays what |
| Car used only for official work | Nil, if the records the rule requires are kept | Keep the log and documents on file |
| Employer-owned house | 10% of salary in cities above 40 lakh population (2011 census), 7.5% in cities of 15 to 40 lakh, 5% elsewhere, minus rent the employee pays | Add 10% a year of the furniture's cost if it is furnished |
| Meals during work hours or paid vouchers usable only at eating places | Nil up to ₹200 a meal | Tax the excess above ₹200 a meal |
| Gifts, vouchers, tokens | Nil if the year's total is below ₹15,000; otherwise the whole amount | Track the running total for each employee |
| Interest-free or cheap loans | Interest at the State Bank of India (SBI) rate on the first day of the tax year, on the maximum monthly balance, minus interest the employee pays | Nil if total loans are ₹2 lakh or less, and for medical treatment of diseases the rules specify |
| ESOPs | Fair market value on the exercise date minus the price paid | Deduct TDS when shares are allotted; eligible start-ups can defer it under section 392(3) |

### The statement of perquisites

Section 392(5)(a) requires the employer to give each employee a statement of perquisites and their values. Under rule 204, it is Form 123 when the employee's salary for the year is more than ₹1,50,000. For lower salaries the details go into Form 130.

## Allowances that only help in the old regime

In the new regime almost every allowance is fully taxable, so structuring pay around allowances saves employees nothing. For employees who choose the old regime, these are the main ones:

- **House rent allowance (HRA).** Exempt up to the least of the HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% of salary in Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad (40% elsewhere). Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad moved to 50% from 1 April 2026. [Chapter 4](/handbooks/tds/investment-declarations/) has a worked example.
- **Leave travel allowance (LTA).** Exempt for travel costs within the rules, against evidence on Form 124.
- **Children's education and hostel allowances.** The 2026 Rules are reported to have raised the exempt limits to ₹3,000 a month per child for education and ₹9,000 a month per child for hostel, for up to two children. Confirm against the notified Rules before you build them into a salary structure.

<Callout type="tip">
For the new regime, the employer's NPS contribution is the one structuring choice that still saves tax. At 14% of basic + DA, an employee with ₹50,000 a month of basic + DA can have ₹84,000 a year contributed without it adding to taxable income. It is retirement money, locked in under NPS rules, so offer it as a choice and explain that.
</Callout>
