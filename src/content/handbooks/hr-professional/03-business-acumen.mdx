SHRM defines business acumen as understanding "the organization's operations, functions and external environment" and applying business tools that keep HR work in line with the company's strategy. You don't need an MBA for this. You need a handful of numbers, the habit of turning people problems into rupees, and a clear way to present them.

## Five numbers to find in your P&L

Ask your finance team for the latest annual and quarterly profit and loss statement (P&L). Staff costs usually sit on a single line, often labelled "Employee benefits expense"; ask finance which line it is in your accounts.

| Number | What it means | How HR moves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | Money earned from customers | Speed and quality of hiring for revenue roles, and how quickly new joiners become productive |
| Employee cost | Salaries, PF, gratuity, bonuses and other staff costs | Headcount, pay design, the mix of employees and contractors |
| Employee cost as a share of revenue | Employee cost ÷ revenue | Productivity, spans of control, automation, attrition |
| Revenue per employee | Revenue ÷ average headcount | Quality of hiring, training and retention |
| Operating profit (EBITDA) | What's left after operating costs, before depreciation, interest and tax | Every people decision moves cost, revenue or both |

Gallup estimates that replacing an employee costs one-half to two times their annual salary, and calls that a conservative estimate. It is based on US data, so treat 0.5 as a cautious starting point rather than an Indian benchmark, and refine it with your own hiring, training and ramp-up costs. The [attrition cost calculator](/toolkit/attrition-cost-calculator/) does the arithmetic for you.

### What a five-point cut in attrition is worth

Using the same example company of 400 people: if attrition falls from 25% to 20%, the company loses 80 people a year instead of 100. That is 20 fewer exits. At ₹12 lakh average CTC and a factor of 0.5, that is 20 × ₹12 lakh × 0.5 = ₹1.2 crore a year. Numbers like this get a retention programme approved.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this now">
Work out the cost of an empty revenue role. If a sales role is expected to bring in ₹1.2 crore a year, each of roughly 250 working days is worth about ₹48,000. A role that stays vacant for 40 extra days costs more than many recruitment budgets.
</Callout>

## Building and defending an HR budget

Build the HR budget from the business plan, not from last year's budget plus a percentage. Start with the headcount plan (the [headcount planning model](/toolkit/headcount-planning-model/) helps), then cost each line from your own data: the HR team, recruitment, learning, HR technology, engagement and wellbeing, and legal and compliance. The [HR budget builder](/toolkit/hr-budget-builder/) gives you a structure.

## Designing salary bands

Without a structure, pay drifts towards whoever negotiated hardest. That is rarely fair, and it can leave men and women doing the same work on different pay, which the Code on Wages prohibits. Salary bands fix this.

1. **Decide your market position.** Pay at the market median for most roles, above it for skills you can't afford to lose, or below it if you offer other value such as employee stock options (ESOPs) or faster learning. Write the choice down.
2. **Build a band for each level.** Each band has a minimum, a midpoint (the pay for someone fully competent in the role) and a maximum. Example: a band for level 3 might run from ₹8 lakh to ₹12 lakh with a midpoint of ₹10 lakh.
3. **Map everyone to a band.** You will find people below the minimum and above the maximum. Raise the first group over two or three cycles. Hold increases for the second group rather than cutting their pay.
4. **Benchmark every year.** Use salary surveys, peer data and job-portal data, and compare like with like: the same city, level, role and pay structure.
5. **Explain the philosophy to managers.** If managers can't explain the bands, employees won't trust them. The [salary band designer](/toolkit/salary-band-designer/) helps you build and check them.

To see where someone sits in their band, use the compa-ratio: salary ÷ band midpoint. Someone on ₹9 lakh in a band with a ₹10 lakh midpoint has a compa-ratio of 0.90.

<Callout type="law" title="CTC, in-hand pay and the Code on Wages">
Two offers with the same CTC can mean very different in-hand pay, depending on PF, gratuity, variable pay and the tax regime. Compare in-hand pay and structure when you benchmark, not CTC alone.

The Labour Codes have been in force since 21 November 2025. Under the Code on Wages, 2019, "wages" means all pay minus a list of exclusions such as HRA and conveyance. If the excluded items add up to more than half of total pay, the excess counts as wages, which feeds into PF, gratuity and other calculations. Read [wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/) and [CTC design](/handbooks/tds/ctc-design-and-perquisites/) before you redesign a structure.
</Callout>

## Presenting to leaders: the SCQA structure

Leaders decide quickly. Put the problem, its cost and your recommendation in the first minute. The situation, complication, question and answer (SCQA) structure, from Barbara Minto's "The Pyramid Principle", is a reliable way to do that.

<Callout type="warning" title="How HR proposals often sound">
"I'd like to propose a new engagement survey. Engagement is important because engaged employees are more productive. We could use a vendor or build our own. The budget would be ₹3 lakh. Can we discuss?" The ask is buried, there is no business case and nothing says why now.
</Callout>
