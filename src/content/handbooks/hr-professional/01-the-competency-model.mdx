## Why HR needs a competency model

A competency model describes what good work looks like, so you can see where you stand and what to learn next. Without one, development tends to be random: a workshop here, a certificate there, and whatever the current job happens to teach.

Three published frameworks are widely used to describe HR competence, each built for its owner's own purpose. All three are useful. None of them covers Indian labour law, payroll or statutory compliance, which is a large part of the job in India.

PeopleCraft's model combines the parts of all three that matter most in Indian workplaces into eight competencies, and the rest of this handbook gives one or more chapters to each.

## The three frameworks at a glance

SHRM (the Society for Human Resource Management) is a US professional body, and its Body of Applied Skills and Knowledge (BASK) defines what its certification exams cover. CIPD (the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development) is a UK professional body; its Profession Map is the basis for its membership grades, qualifications and learning pathways. AIHR (the Academy to Innovate HR) is an online HR learning provider; it suggests using its T-Shaped HR Competency Model to assess skills, describe roles and plan development.

### What changed recently

- **SHRM:** the 2026 BASK combined two behavioural competencies, Inclusion & Diversity and Global Mindset, into one called Inclusive Mindset, and added AI examples throughout. Earlier editions had nine behavioural competencies, so older study material may not match.
- **CIPD:** the November 2025 review added Workforce Planning as a specialist knowledge area and added fact and source checking to evidence-based practice in response to AI. CIPD added standards on AI to its "Technology and people" area in 2023.
- **AIHR:** the current model has six core competencies, including AI Fluency.

Titles vary a lot between companies. A "manager" in a 40-person startup may do work that a larger company would give to an executive, and the reverse is also common. Place yourself by what you do on an ordinary day.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this now">
Look at your last two weeks of work. Count how much time went on carrying out processes, owning them, shaping decisions or designing systems. The biggest share is your current stage, whatever your title says.
</Callout>

## Certifications: what they give you

Certifications give structure to your learning and a signal employers recognise. They don't replace judgement in a real conversation, and none of the three bodies' credentials is a substitute for knowing Indian labour law.

- **SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP.** SHRM's exams are built on the BASK. SHRM describes the SHRM-CP as being for professionals in operational roles and the SHRM-SCP for senior professionals in strategic roles.
- **CIPD qualifications and membership.** CIPD offers foundation, associate and advanced qualifications, and its membership grades are aligned to the four levels of the Profession Map. After a qualification you can progress to Chartered Membership or Chartered Fellowship depending on your experience.
- **AIHR courses.** AIHR publishes its model with a self-assessment and runs online courses focused on skills such as analytics and digital HR.

Fees, eligibility rules and exam formats change, and some are priced in US dollars or pounds. Check the current details on each body's website before you plan your budget, and look at real job postings for the roles you want to see which credentials employers there mention.
