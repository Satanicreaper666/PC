All three frameworks now treat technology and AI as core HR skills. AIHR lists Digital Agility and AI Fluency among its six core competencies. SHRM's 2026 BASK added AI examples throughout. CIPD added standards on responsible AI use to its "Technology and people" knowledge area in 2023. You don't need to code. You need to know what to automate, how to choose tools, and when to be sceptical.

## The HR technology stack

| Category | What it does | When you need it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HR system (HRMS or HRIS) | Employee records, leave, attendance, documents | From the first few hires; often bundled with payroll |
| Payroll | Salary, provident fund (PF), Employees' State Insurance (ESI), professional tax, tax deducted at source (TDS), payslips and returns | From the first hire, in-house or through a provider |
| Applicant tracking (ATS) | Job posts, candidate pipeline, interview feedback | When you hire regularly |
| Learning (LMS) | Courses, compulsory training, completion records | When you run regular training programmes |
| Performance and engagement | Goals, check-ins, reviews, surveys | When managers need structure and you want regular feedback |
| Analytics | Dashboards that combine the systems above | When the data in the other systems is clean |

### What to check before you buy

- **Data export and exit terms.** Can you take all your data out, in a usable format, if you leave?
- **Integration.** Does it connect with your payroll, attendance and hiring tools, or will someone re-type data every month?
- **Statutory updates.** How quickly did the vendor update for the Labour Codes, the new EPF wage ceiling and the Income-tax Act, 2025? Ask for dates.
- **Security and access.** Who at the vendor can see your data, where is it stored, and can you limit access by role?
- **Data protection readiness.** What will the vendor do to help you meet the DPDP Act's duties?
- **Real cost.** Price per employee per year, plus implementation, training and add-ons.

## Where AI helps HR, and where it needs care

AI tools are good at language: drafting, summarising, grouping and answering routine questions. They are not accountable for decisions, and they can repeat the biases in the data they learned from.

<Callout type="law" title="Existing law applies to AI tools">
Whatever tool you use, the employer remains responsible. The Code on Wages, 2019 (section 3) bars discrimination on the ground of sex when recruiting for the same or similar work, and an AI tool trained on past hiring can repeat old patterns. The DPDP Act will govern the personal data these tools process. Ask vendors how they test for bias, and keep records of how decisions were made.
</Callout>

### Rules for your team's use of AI

- Don't paste personal, salary, health or disciplinary data into public AI tools. Use tools your company has approved for that data, if any.
- Check every output for accuracy, especially anything legal or numerical. AI tools state wrong answers confidently.
- Tell candidates and employees when AI is used in a process that affects them.
- Keep a person accountable for every decision about someone's job, pay or rights.

## Preparing for the DPDP Act

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 will change how employers handle the personal data of candidates, employees and former employees. It is coming into force in phases.

### What to do before 2027

1. **Map your employee data.** List what you collect, why, where it is stored, who can see it and how long you keep it. Include candidates and former employees.
2. **Trim your forms.** Look hard at joining forms. If no law or real business need requires a field, such as religion or blood group, stop collecting it. The [employee joining form](/toolkit/employee-joining-form/) is a starting point.
3. **Work out the legal basis for each use.** The Act allows processing with consent and for certain "legitimate uses" set out in section 7. Don't assume every use of employee data needs fresh consent, or that none does; take advice on each use.
4. **Set retention periods.** Keep records as long as a law or a real need requires, then delete them.
5. **Plan for a breach.** Decide who does what if employee data leaks, and how you will tell the people affected and the authorities, as the Rules require.
6. **Check your vendors.** Payroll providers, HR systems and background-check agencies process your employees' data on your behalf. Your contracts should say how they protect it.
