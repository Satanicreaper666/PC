The idea of HR as a partner to the business is usually traced to Dave Ulrich's work in the late 1990s, including his 1998 Harvard Business Review article "A New Mandate for Human Resources". SHRM's BASK now describes the skill as Consultation: working with stakeholders on business challenges and building support for HR solutions. The challenge is rarely knowing this. It is having the time, the credibility and the confidence to do it.

## The four modes of an HRBP

An effective HRBP switches between four modes, depending on what the business needs that week. This is PeopleCraft's practical framing.

| Mode | When you are in it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic partner | Planning, organisation design, growth, mergers | Building next year's workforce plan with the business head |
| Employee champion | Engagement, culture, careers, fairness | Making the case for a flexible-work pilot |
| Operations manager | Processes, compliance, audits | Redesigning the onboarding process |
| Crisis responder | Complaints, sudden exits, incidents | Handling a harassment complaint or a team walk-out |

All four matter. The trap is spending nearly all your time in the bottom two. You can't be strategic while you process leave requests by hand.

<Callout type="tip" title="How to win back time">
Write down the procedure for anything you do more than twice a month. Move routine questions to a self-service page or chatbot. Automate approvals and standard letters. Then hand day-to-day tasks to a junior colleague with the procedure, and review weekly at first.
</Callout>

## Mapping your stakeholders

Not every stakeholder needs the same attention. Map them by how much influence they have over your work and how interested they are in people issues.

| Stakeholder | What they want from HR | How to serve them | Mistake to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CEO or founder | Talent that drives growth, a culture that scales, no compliance surprises | A short quarterly people dashboard and early warning of risks | Bringing them operational detail |
| Finance head | Cost control, justified headcount, statutory compliance | An HR budget with expected returns; cost per hire and attrition cost | Asking for money without a business case |
| Line managers | Good hires quickly, a stable team, help with poor performance | Hiring timelines, manager toolkits, coaching on hard conversations | Policing policy instead of solving problems |
| Employees | Fair pay, growth, respect, someone to talk to | Clear policies, visible action on feedback, an open door | Being seen as management's informer |
| Board and investors | Organisational health, key-person risk, succession | A people section in the quarterly review | Not having the data when asked |

## Your first 90 days in a new HRBP role

The same plan works if you are the first and only HR person in a growing company. The [HRBP first 90 days plan](/toolkit/hrbp-first-90-days-plan/) turns it into a checklist.

<Figure title="The first 90 days" caption="Read in order. Each phase ends with something you can show: a written problem list, visible fixes, then an agreed plan." wide>
  <Timeline
    items={[
      { when: "Day 1", label: "Start listening" },
      { when: "Day 30", label: "Top five people problems written up" },
      { when: "Day 60", label: "Two or three visible fixes done" },
      { when: "Day 90", label: "Six-month people plan agreed", accent: true }
    ]}
    spans={[
      { from: 0, to: 1, label: "Listen" },
      { from: 1, to: 2, label: "Quick wins" },
      { from: 2, to: 3, label: "Plan" }
    ]}
  />
</Figure>

- **Days 1 to 30, listen.** Hold one-to-ones with every leader and with a cross-section of employees at different levels and locations. Learn the business: products, customers, targets, the P&L. Write down the five people problems you hear most.
- **Days 31 to 60, quick wins.** Fix two or three problems everyone can see, such as a payroll error that keeps recurring, a missing policy or an employee relations case that has been stuck for months. Credibility comes from results.
- **Days 61 to 90, plan.** Present a six-month people plan to the leadership team: priorities, measures, budget and timeline. Ask for agreement on the top three.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this now">
Can you name the three business priorities of the unit you support, such as the revenue target, a product launch or a new market? Business priorities, not HR priorities. If not, book 30 minutes with your business head this week.
</Callout>

## Running a quarterly people review

A quarterly people review gives people issues the same discipline a sales review gives revenue. Keep it to an hour, send a short pre-read, and end with agreed actions and owners.

<Figure title="The quarterly people review rhythm" caption="The cycle repeats every quarter. Actions agreed in the meeting are tracked until the next review.">
  <Cycle
    center="Every quarter"
    items={[
      { title: "Gather data", note: "Week 1: HR systems, finance, surveys" },
      { title: "Find the story", note: "Week 2: causes, not just counts" },
      { title: "Send a pre-read", note: "Week 3: two pages, three asks" },
      { title: "Hold the review", note: "Week 4: decide and assign owners" },
      { title: "Track actions", note: "Rest of the quarter" }
    ]}
  />
</Figure>

Cover five areas in the review:

1. **Headcount and cost.** Headcount against plan, employee cost as a share of revenue, open roles and the hiring pipeline.
2. **Attrition and retention.** Voluntary and total attrition, regretted exits and the main reasons from exit interviews.
3. **Engagement.** Employee net promoter score (eNPS) trend, manager scores and themes from surveys or skip-level meetings.
4. **Talent.** Succession cover for critical roles, high-potential people and flight risks.
5. **Risks and recommendations.** Compliance gaps, culture concerns and busy periods ahead, with your top three recommendations and their expected effect.
