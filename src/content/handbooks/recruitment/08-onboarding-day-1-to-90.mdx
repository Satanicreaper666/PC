Onboarding decides whether a good hire becomes a productive, committed colleague or an early exit. It starts the day the offer is accepted, not the day the person walks in.

## Keep the candidate close through the notice period

In India the gap between acceptance and joining is often one to three months, set by the candidate's current contract. Their employer may make a counter-offer, and other companies are still calling. Stay in touch without pressure.

<Callout type="tip" title="If a counter-offer arrives">
Don't panic and don't automatically match it. Ask what has changed for them, remind them why they chose your role, confirm what you've committed to and give them space to decide. Someone who stays only for money often keeps looking.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Respect their current employer">
Don't ask a new hire to start work, join internal meetings or share anything from their current employer before they join. Introduce them to the team by email, and add them to informal chat groups only if they want to be added.
</Callout>

## Plan day 1 and week 1

Day 1 should feel like arriving somewhere that expected you. Keep it for people and purpose; spread the forms across the week.

By the end of week 1 the person should have met the people they'll work with most, used the main tools, finished one small real task and read the policies that matter (leave, conduct, POSH, IT and data).

## Complete the joining compliance

| Item | What to do | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appointment letter | Issue it on joining, in the format your appropriate government prescribes, and keep the signed acknowledgement | Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020, section 6(1)(f) |
| Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) | If the establishment is covered, enrol the employee; if they already have a Universal Account Number (UAN), link the new employment to it | Code on Social Security, 2020 and the EPF Scheme |
| Employees' State Insurance (ESI) | Register the employee if the establishment is covered and their wages are within the ceiling | Code on Social Security, 2020 |
| Nominations | Collect PF and gratuity nominations | Code on Social Security, 2020 and its rules |
| Tax | Collect the employee's tax declarations, and salary and tax deducted at source (TDS) details from any previous employer in the same tax year | See [joiners and leavers](/handbooks/tds/joiners-leavers-and-transfers/) and [investment declarations](/handbooks/tds/investment-declarations/) |
| POSH | Share the policy and how to reach the Internal Committee | See [POSH compliance](/handbooks/labour-law/posh-compliance/) |
| Documents | Check originals and hand them back the same day; keep copies only of what you need | Good practice and data minimisation |

<Callout type="law" title="The appointment letter format">
The OSH Code requires a letter of appointment for every employee, in the form the appropriate government prescribes. For establishments under the central government, the OSH (Central) Rules, 2026 set a format that covers, among other items, designation, type of employment, date of joining, wages and allowances, EPF and ESI details, broad duties and maternity benefits for women employees. It asks for Aadhaar only with the employee's consent. Most private employers follow their state's format. Use the [appointment letter](/toolkit/appointment-letter/) template and the [employee joining form](/toolkit/employee-joining-form/).
</Callout>

## Share a 30-60-90 day plan

Write the plan before the person joins, from the outcomes in the [one-sentence case](/handbooks/recruitment/do-you-need-this-role/) and the job description. Share it on day 1, agree it together and use it as the basis of the probation review.

| Phase | Focus | Example deliverables | Manager's part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 30: learn | The product, the team, the customers, how work gets done | Shadow three colleagues, finish the onboarding checklist, sit in on five customer calls | Weekly 30-minute 1:1; no pressure to deliver yet |
| Days 31 to 60: contribute | Own small pieces of work | Finish two or three tasks independently; present one finding or idea to the team | 1:1 every two weeks: what's working, what's confusing |
| Days 61 to 90: own | Run their area with less support | Deliver one key objective; build working relationships with key stakeholders | 90-day review with written feedback |

## Spot early warning signs and act early

Watch the work, not the person's private life, and ask rather than assume. Changes in behaviour can have causes outside work, such as health or family, where support matters more than pressure.

| When | Signs | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Quiet in meetings, not asking questions | Buddy check-in and an informal chat with the manager |
| Weeks 3 to 4 | Missed deadlines, unclear about priorities, not using the tools | A structured 1:1 with a written task list |
| Month 2 | Still needs a lot of help, avoids feedback | An honest conversation: “Is the role what you expected?” |
| Month 3 | Disengaged, more unplanned absence | Decide together on more support, a clear improvement plan or a fair parting |

<Callout type="law" title="Probation rules">
For most employees, the employment contract and company policy set the probation period. Where standing orders apply (industrial establishments with 300 or more workers), the Model Standing Orders, 2026 set probation at six months, extendable by three. Before ending employment during probation, check the contract, any standing orders and your state's shops and establishments law, and see [termination and disputes](/handbooks/labour-law/termination-grievance-and-disputes/).
</Callout>

If the answer is an improvement plan rather than an extension, use the [performance improvement plan](/toolkit/performance-improvement-plan/) and the [onboarding checklist](/toolkit/onboarding-checklist/) to keep everything on record.
