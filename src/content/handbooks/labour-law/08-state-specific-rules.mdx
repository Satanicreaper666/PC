## Why state rules still matter

Labour is a subject on which both Parliament and the states make laws. The four Codes are central laws, but each Code lets the "appropriate government" make rules, and for most private businesses that is the state. So the state decides details such as spread-over, overtime caps, register formats, the age for free health check-ups and many forms.

Under the Social Security Code, the Centre is the appropriate government for establishments with branches in more than one state, and it runs EPF and ESI for everyone. States also keep their own laws that the Codes didn't replace: Shops and Establishments Acts, national and festival holiday Acts, professional tax and labour welfare funds.

## Where state rules stood in 2026

The Centre notified its final rules on 8–9 May 2026. States moved at different speeds.

- **States and union territories with final rules under one or more Codes by late September 2026** (as reported): Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand. Several had notified rules under only some of the four Codes.
- **Still on draft rules in the trackers we checked:** several large states, including Karnataka and Maharashtra. Lists change month to month, so check your own state.
- **Target:** the Labour Secretary said in September 2026 that the Centre hoped every state and union territory would publish rules by 31 October 2026.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check before you rely on this list">
Rule status changes month by month, and trackers don't always agree. Before changing a policy, look up the notification on your state labour department's website or in the state gazette, and note the date it takes effect.
</Callout>

## Shops and Establishments Acts

Each state has a Shops and Establishments Act covering shops, offices and other commercial establishments: registration, hours, weekly off, leave, holidays and conditions for women's night work. The Codes didn't repeal these Acts. Several states have started aligning them with the OSH Code:

- **Karnataka:** from 4 September 2026, establishments with 10 or more workers registered under the OSH Code are excluded from the Shops and Commercial Establishments Act. Commentators differ on whether this removes the whole Act or only its registration duty, so take advice before dropping its leave and notice rules.
- **Maharashtra and Haryana:** establishments registered under the OSH Code don't need separate Shops Act registration (April and May 2026). Maharashtra still asks businesses with fewer than 10 workers to report when they start. A 2025 ordinance raised Maharashtra's threshold to 20 workers and allowed 10-hour days; check whether the replacing Bill has become law.
- **Assam, Chhattisgarh and Andaman and Nicobar Islands** made similar changes in 2026, and Bihar repealed its 2025 Shops Act.

For workers covered by the OSH Code, the Code's annual leave rule also applies ([Leave and working hours](/handbooks/labour-law/leave-and-working-hours/)). Where a state law gives more, employers usually follow the more generous rule.

## Professional tax

Professional tax (PT) is a state tax on employment that the employer deducts from salary and pays to the state where the employee works. The Constitution caps it at ₹2,500 per person per year. Examples for salaried employees, by monthly salary:

- **Karnataka:** nil below ₹25,000; from ₹25,000, ₹200 a month and ₹300 in February (₹2,500 a year).
- **Maharashtra:** for men, nil up to ₹7,500, ₹175 a month up to ₹10,000, and ₹200 a month above that (₹300 in February). For women, nil up to ₹25,000 and ₹200 a month above that (₹300 in February).
- **Gujarat:** nil up to ₹12,000; ₹200 a month above.
- **West Bengal, from October 2026:** nil up to ₹20,000; ₹100 a month up to ₹30,000; ₹140 up to ₹50,000; ₹170 up to ₹1,00,000; ₹208 above.
- **Telangana and Andhra Pradesh:** nil up to ₹15,000; ₹150 a month up to ₹20,000; ₹200 above.
- **Tamil Nadu (Greater Chennai Corporation):** charged on half-yearly salary in six slabs, from nil up to ₹21,000 a half-year to ₹1,250 a half-year above ₹75,000.

As reported in 2026, Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir and most union territories don't levy PT on salaries, and Odisha stopped from 1 April 2026. Due dates and returns differ by state. The [professional tax calculator](/toolkit/professional-tax-calculator/) covers the main states.

## Labour welfare fund

About 16 states and union territories run a labour welfare fund, including Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Delhi and Telangana. Contributions are fixed amounts set by each state's welfare board, most often due half-yearly, in June and December. Boards revise the amounts, so take them from the latest board notification.

## Minimum wages

Each state notifies minimum wages for its establishments, by skill category and often by zone, and revises them from time to time. Once the Centre notifies a national floor wage, no state rate can be below it. See [Wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/).
