## What the four Codes are

Parliament passed four Labour Codes in 2019 and 2020 to replace 29 central labour laws. The government brought all four into force on 21 November 2025. Each Code folds several older Acts into one law with common definitions, so terms such as "wages", "worker" and "employee" now mean much the same thing across payroll, safety and disputes.

| Code | What it covers | Laws replaced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code on Wages, 2019 | Minimum wages, payment of wages, deductions, bonus, equal pay | 4 |
| Industrial Relations Code, 2020 | Unions, standing orders, grievances, strikes, lay-off, retrenchment, closure, disputes | 3 |
| Code on Social Security, 2020 | EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit, employees' compensation, gig and platform workers | 9 |
| Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020 | Registration, safety, health, welfare, hours, leave, women's employment, contract labour, migrant workers | 13 |

The laws each Code replaced:

- **Code on Wages:** Payment of Wages Act 1936, Minimum Wages Act 1948, Payment of Bonus Act 1965 and Equal Remuneration Act 1976.
- **Industrial Relations Code:** Trade Unions Act 1926, Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 and Industrial Disputes Act 1947.
- **Code on Social Security:** EPF Act 1952, ESI Act 1948, Employees' Compensation Act 1923, Employment Exchanges Act 1959, Maternity Benefit Act 1961, Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, Cine-Workers Welfare Fund Act 1981, Building Workers' Welfare Cess Act 1996 and Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act 2008.
- **OSH Code:** Factories Act 1948, Plantations Labour Act 1951, Mines Act 1952, the two Working Journalists Acts (1955 and 1958), Motor Transport Workers Act 1961, Beedi and Cigar Workers Act 1966, Contract Labour Act 1970, Sales Promotion Employees Act 1976, Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979, Cine-Workers and Cinema Theatre Workers Act 1981, Dock Workers Act 1986 and Building and Other Construction Workers Act 1996.

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act, 2013 and each state's Shops and Establishments Act are not part of the Codes. Both still apply. See [POSH compliance](/handbooks/labour-law/posh-compliance/) and [state rules](/handbooks/labour-law/state-specific-rules/).

### How the transition works

The Codes did not switch off every old rule overnight. The Code on Wages says rules and notifications made under the repealed Acts stay in force, to the extent they are not contrary to the Code, until they are replaced. The Labour Ministry's FAQs say the same for all four Codes: old rules remain until new rules are notified. The Social Security Code keeps schemes and regulations under the old Acts alive for one year, to 20 November 2026. The EPF schemes were replaced on 29 June 2026; replacements for the old ESI regulations were still awaited at the time of writing.

<Callout type="note" title="State rules are the moving part">
By late September 2026, about ten states had notified final rules under one or more Codes, and the Centre said it hoped every state and union territory would publish rules by 31 October 2026. Several large states, including Karnataka and Maharashtra, were still on draft rules in the trackers we checked. Check your state's labour department or gazette before relying on any list, including ours.
</Callout>

## Which rules apply to your establishment

The Codes are central laws, but for most private businesses the state is the "appropriate government" that makes the detailed rules on wages, hours, leave and working conditions. The Centre makes rules for its own sectors, such as mines, major ports and central public sector undertakings. It runs EPF and ESI for everyone, and under the Social Security Code it is also the appropriate government for establishments with branches in more than one state.

<Callout type="warning" title="The '50% basic pay' rule is a myth">
The Codes don't say basic pay must be half of CTC. They say that if certain excluded items, such as HRA, conveyance and commission, add up to more than half of total pay, the excess counts as wages for PF, gratuity and other benefits. [Wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/) works through an example.
</Callout>
