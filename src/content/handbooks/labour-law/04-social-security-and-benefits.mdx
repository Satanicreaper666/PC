## EPF: who is covered and what is paid

EPF applies to every establishment with 20 or more employees. Since 29 June 2026 it runs under the Employees' Provident Fund Scheme, 2026, the Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026 and the Employees' Deposit-Linked Insurance Scheme, 2026, made under the Social Security Code. They replaced the 1952, 1995 and 1976 schemes.

**The wage ceiling** decides who must join. It was ₹15,000 a month until 16 September 2026 and became **₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026** (notification S.O. 5109(E)). The Union Cabinet approved the change on 16 September 2026.

- Employees earning up to ₹25,000 when they would become members must be enrolled. Employees earning between ₹15,000 and ₹25,000 who were outside PF had to be enrolled from 17 September 2026.
- Someone earning more than ₹25,000 at that point is an "excluded employee". They can join with the employer's agreement.
- Existing members can't opt out because their pay rises.
- "Wages" for PF follows the Social Security Code definition, including the 50% add-back in [Wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/).

Contributions and the electronic challan-cum-return (ECR) are due by the 15th of the following month. Late payment carries simple interest of 12% a year from the due date. A few classes of establishment, such as those under an insolvency resolution plan and jute, beedi and brick units, pay 10% instead of 12%. The [EPF calculator](/toolkit/epf-calculator/) works out the split.

<Callout type="note">
September 2026 was split: contributions for 1–16 September used the ₹15,000 ceiling and 17–30 September used ₹25,000, in one return due by 15 October 2026.
</Callout>

## ESI: who is covered and what is paid

ESI now applies across India, not just in notified areas. It covers establishments with 10 or more employees, is voluntary for smaller ones, and is mandatory where even one employee works in a hazardous or life-threatening process.

- **Rates:** 0.75% of wages from the employee and 3.25% from the employer, each rounded up to the next rupee. Employees with an average daily wage of ₹176 or less pay no employee share; the employer still pays. This figure comes from secondary sources, so confirm it with ESIC.
- **Staying covered:** someone covered at the start of a contribution period stays covered until it ends, even if their pay rises above the ceiling.
- **Wages:** the Social Security Code definition applies from 21 November 2025, so the 50% add-back can bring someone under the ceiling or raise their contribution.

<Callout type="warning" title="ESI rules are changing">
The Social Security Code keeps the old ESI regulations in force only until 20 November 2026. Draft replacement regulations were reported in August 2026. Check ESIC's latest notifications before November payroll, and use the [ESI calculator](/toolkit/esi-calculator/) only with the current ceiling.
</Callout>

## Gratuity

Gratuity is paid when employment ends after at least five years of continuous service: on retirement, superannuation, resignation, death or disablement, or when a fixed-term contract ends. The five-year minimum doesn't apply on death or disablement. **Fixed-term employees** qualify after one year of service under the contract, paid in proportion to service.

A year of continuous service generally means 240 days worked in the preceding 12 months (190 days below ground in mines or where the establishment works fewer than six days a week). Working journalists qualify after three years.

- **Ceiling:** gratuity can't exceed the amount the Centre notifies, which the Labour Ministry's FAQs confirm is currently <Fact id="gratuity.ceiling" />.
- **Wages:** gratuity uses the Social Security Code definition with the 50% add-back. The Ministry's FAQs say this applies from 21 November 2025 and that the amount is worked out on the last drawn wages.
- **Payment:** within 30 days of the date it becomes payable. Delay attracts simple interest.
- **Nomination and forfeiture:** employees file a nomination in the form and time the rules set. Gratuity can be forfeited only to the extent of damage the employee caused, or wholly or partly for riotous or violent conduct or an offence involving moral turpitude committed in the course of employment.

Use the [gratuity calculator](/toolkit/gratuity-calculator/) for a real exit.

<Callout type="tip" title="Budget for fixed-term gratuity">
Example: 200 fixed-term staff on wages of ₹25,000 a month each build up ₹14,423 of gratuity for every completed year, about ₹28.8 lakh a year for the group if their contracts run at least a year. Provide for it monthly rather than at the end of each contract.
</Callout>

## Gig and platform workers

The Social Security Code recognises gig workers and platform workers for the first time and lets the Centre frame schemes for life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity benefits, old-age protection and crèches.

- **Aggregator contribution:** between 1% and 2% of annual turnover, capped at 5% of the amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers. The Centre notifies the rate and the date contributions start.
- **Registration:** under the central rules, workers aged 16 and above register on the Centre's portal. Aggregators had to share details of existing workers within 45 days of the rules starting and must register new workers as they join.
- **Eligibility for scheme benefits:** 90 days with one aggregator, or 120 days across several, in the previous financial year.
- **Status:** the welfare schemes and the start date for aggregator contributions were still awaited in the latest updates we checked. Some states have their own laws, such as Telangana's Platform Based Gig Workers Act, 2026, in force from 2 June 2026.

<Callout type="warning" title="Classify people carefully">
Calling someone a gig worker doesn't make them one. If a person works under your direction like an employee, treat them as one for PF, ESI and the other Codes, and take advice where the line is unclear.
</Callout>

## Disputes about dues

EPFO and ESIC officers can decide whether the Code applies and how much an employer owes. They can't start proceedings more than five years after the amount became due, and should finish an inquiry within two years. Penalties for not paying are in [Penalties](/handbooks/labour-law/penalties/).
