## Working hours, breaks and overtime

These limits protect "workers" in establishments covered by the OSH Code, which generally means 10 or more workers. A "worker" excludes people employed mainly in managerial or administrative roles and supervisors earning more than ₹18,000 a month.

| Rule | Limit | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily hours | 8 hours | OSH Code s.25 |
| Weekly hours | 48 hours | OSH (Central) Rules 2026 |
| Days a week | No more than 6; a weekly holiday | OSH Code s.26 |
| Rest break | At least 30 minutes after no more than 5 hours of continuous work | Notification under OSH Code s.25, May 2026 |
| Spread-over (first start to last finish) | Set by notification; state rules commonly allow 12 hours | State rules |
| Overtime cap | 144 hours a quarter under the central rules; states set 115 to 150 | Central and state rules |

The Code on Wages central rules also require a weekly rest day and no more than 10 days in a row without one. Longer days in a shorter week, such as four days of 12 hours, are possible where the applicable rules allow them and the 48-hour weekly limit is kept, but no employer has to offer them.

### Working out overtime pay

Overtime is paid at twice the ordinary rate and needs the worker's consent. For a monthly-paid worker the daily rate is the monthly wage divided by 26. Overtime of 15 to 30 minutes counts as half an hour, and more than 30 minutes counts as an hour.

Some states now exempt establishments registered under the OSH Code from their Shops Act; Karnataka's amendment took effect on 4 September 2026. - **Rate:** one day for every 20 days worked; one for every 15 for adolescents and for workers below ground in mines. For example, a worker who works 240 days earns 12 days.
- **Carry-forward:** up to 30 days into the next calendar year. Leave a worker applied for and was refused can be carried forward without that limit.
- **Encashment:** a worker can ask for unused leave to be encashed at the end of the calendar year. When a worker is discharged or dismissed, or quits, unused leave is paid within two working days; on superannuation or death, within two months.
- **Records:** keep a leave register in the prescribed form.

The [leave encashment calculator](/toolkit/leave-encashment-calculator/) works out the amount, and the [leave policy](/toolkit/leave-policy/) template sets the rules out for employees.

## Casual leave, sick leave and holidays

No national law sets a minimum number of casual or sick leave days for private employers. These come from the state Shops and Establishments Act, standing orders where they apply, and your policy. Paid national and festival holidays are set by state law. Two examples:

- **Karnataka:** up to 12 days a year of paid leave for sickness, accident or other reasonable cause, annual leave carry-forward of up to 45 days, and 10 paid holidays, five of them fixed (26 January, 1 May, 15 August, 2 October and 1 November).
- **Maharashtra:** 8 days of casual leave a year, earned leave that can accumulate up to 45 days, and 8 paid festival holidays, four of them fixed (26 January, 1 May, 15 August and 2 October).

Where a state law gives more than the OSH Code, employers usually apply the more generous rule. How the Codes and state Acts fit together is not fully settled, so check your state ([state rules](/handbooks/labour-law/state-specific-rules/)).

## Maternity, adoption and paternity

A woman who has worked at least 80 days in the 12 months before her expected delivery is entitled to paid maternity benefit at her average daily wage.

- **Adoption and surrogacy:** adoptive and commissioning mothers get 12 weeks from the day the child is handed over. Since the Supreme Court's ruling of 17 March 2026, this applies whatever the adopted child's age; the earlier "below three months" condition was struck down.
- **Other paid leave:** six weeks after a miscarriage or medical termination, two weeks after a tubectomy, and up to one extra month for illness arising from pregnancy.
- **Medical bonus:** ₹3,500 (or a higher amount if the Centre notifies one) where the employer doesn't provide free pre-natal and post-natal care.
- **Work from home:** after the maternity benefit ends, the employer may allow work from home on agreed terms where the nature of the work permits.
- **Telling women about it:** every woman must be told about these benefits in writing and electronically when she is appointed.
- **ESI-covered women** draw maternity benefit from ESI instead, subject to its contribution conditions.

**Paternity leave:** no central law requires private employers to give it, so it is a policy choice. Central government employees get 15 days, a common benchmark.

## Crèche

An establishment with 50 or more employees must provide a crèche for children under six, generally within one kilometre, and allow four visits a day. The Ministry has clarified that the crèche is for employees of either gender. Under the central rules, where there is no crèche, employees can agree to a crèche allowance of at least ₹500 a month per child, for up to two children. Some commentators question whether rules can replace the statutory duty with an allowance, so take advice before relying on it.

## Night shifts for women

Women can be employed in all establishments and all types of work. They can work before 6 am and after 7 pm only with their consent and on the conditions the appropriate government sets. The central rules require written consent, safe transport from home, well-lit washrooms, drinking water and CCTV in the specified areas, and compliance with the POSH Act. State rules set their own conditions, so check yours.

## Health check-ups

The OSH Code requires a free annual health check-up for employees of the ages and classes the rules prescribe. The Central Rules 2026 apply it to employees aged 40 and above in dock work, mines, and building or construction work, plus any classes notified later. Some state rules, many still in draft, go wider, for example all workers aged 40 or 45 and above, so check your state's final rules. ESI facilities can be used where available.

## Welfare facilities and safety staff

| Facility or role | When the OSH Code requires it |
| --- | --- |
| First-aid boxes or cupboards | Every establishment, available during working hours |
| Canteen | 100 or more workers ordinarily employed, counting contract workers |
| Crèche | More than 50 workers (and 50 or more employees under the Social Security Code) |
| Welfare officer | Factories, mines and plantations with 250 or more workers |
| Safety officer | Factories with 500 or more workers, or 250 with a hazardous process; construction sites with 250 or more; mines with 100 or more |
| Safety committee | Where the government requires one by notification; state rules commonly set 500 workers |

Every employee also has duties under the Code: to take reasonable care of their own and others' safety, follow the safety standards, cooperate with the employer, report unsafe conditions and not interfere with safety equipment.
