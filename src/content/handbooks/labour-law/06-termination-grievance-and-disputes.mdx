## Ways employment ends

| How it ends | What the law requires | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resignation | Notice as per the contract; wages due within two working days of leaving | [Wages and pay](/handbooks/labour-law/wages-and-pay/) |
| Dismissal for misconduct | A fair process under your standing orders or rules; subsistence allowance during suspension | Below |
| Retrenchment | One month's notice or pay, compensation, notice to government, re-skilling fund | Below |
| End of a fixed-term contract | No notice on expiry; gratuity if one year or more | [Contracts](/handbooks/labour-law/contracts-and-fixed-term-employment/) |
| Lay-off | Temporary; compensation at half of basic and DA | Below |
| Closure | 60 days' notice to the government where 50 or more workers | Below |

No law sets a general notice period for employees who resign from a private employer; it comes from the contract or the standing orders. Notice buy-outs and garden leave are contractual too. Retrenchment rules protect "workers", which excludes people employed mainly in managerial or administrative roles and supervisors earning more than ₹18,000 a month.

State Shops and Establishments Acts can add employer-side notice. For example, where Karnataka's Act still applies, an employee with at least six months' continuous service can be dismissed only with one month's notice or pay, unless misconduct is proved at an inquiry.

## Retrenchment

Retrenchment means the employer ending a worker's service for any reason other than disciplinary punishment. It doesn't include voluntary retirement, superannuation, the expiry or non-renewal of a contract, the end of a fixed-term tenure, or termination for continued ill health. A worker with at least one year of continuous service can be retrenched only if the employer:

1. gives one month's written notice stating the reasons, or pays wages for the notice period instead;
2. pays compensation of 15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service, counting a part-year of more than six months as a year; and
3. serves notice on the appropriate government or the authority it names.

Unless there is an agreement otherwise, the last person employed in a category is normally the first retrenched; recording reasons is required to depart from that. If the employer hires again within a year, retrenched workers who are Indian citizens get the chance to apply and have preference.

### The worker re-skilling fund

For each worker retrenched, the employer contributes 15 days of the worker's last drawn wages to the worker re-skilling fund. Under the central rules the employer transfers it within 10 days of the retrenchment, with the worker's details. The fund then credits the same amount to the worker's bank account within 45 days of the retrenchment. This is on top of the compensation, not part of it.

## When government permission is needed

Chapter X of the Industrial Relations Code requires prior permission from the appropriate government before a lay-off, retrenchment or closure. It covers only some establishments.

The threshold was 100 workers under the Industrial Disputes Act. A state can notify a number higher than 300 for establishments in its sphere, so check your state's notification.

## Lay-off and closure

**Lay-off** is a temporary inability to give work, for example because of a shortage of power or raw materials. A laid-off worker with at least one year of continuous service gets compensation of 50% of basic wages and dearness allowance for the days laid off. Under an agreement, compensation can stop after the first 45 days of lay-off in any 12 months. These rules apply to factories, mines and plantations with 50 or more workers on average, excluding seasonal and intermittent work.

**Closure:** an employer that intends to close an undertaking must give the appropriate government at least 60 days' notice with reasons. This applies where 50 or more workers were employed on any day in the previous 12 months, and not to construction projects. Chapter X establishments also need permission.

## Grievances

Every industrial establishment with 20 or more workers must have one or more grievance redressal committees of up to 10 members, with equal numbers from the employer and the workers. Women must be represented at least in proportion to their share of the workforce. Establishments with 100 or more workers may also be required to set up a works committee.

Industrial Tribunals have two members, one judicial and one administrative. After a conciliation report, either party can apply to the Tribunal within 90 days of receiving it.

## Strikes, lock-outs and unions

No one in an industrial establishment may go on strike:

- without giving notice within 60 days before the strike;
- within 14 days of giving that notice, or before the date it names;
- while conciliation is pending and for 7 days after it ends;
- while Tribunal or arbitration proceedings are pending and for 60 days after they end; or
- on matters covered by a settlement or award that is in force.

Similar conditions apply to lock-outs. A "strike" includes concerted casual leave taken on the same day by 50% or more of the workers. Taking part in, encouraging or funding an illegal strike or lock-out can lead to fines and imprisonment for up to a month.

Where there are several registered unions, a union with 51% or more of the workers on the muster roll is recognised as the sole negotiating union. If none reaches 51%, a negotiating council is formed, with one seat for every 20% of support. Recognition lasts three years.

**Changing service conditions** such as wages, hours or leave needs at least 21 days' notice to the affected workers before the change takes effect.

## Discipline and suspension

The law doesn't prescribe a step-by-step inquiry for every employer, but standing orders do where they apply, and a fair process is what protects a decision if it is challenged.

If a worker is suspended pending an inquiry, the inquiry should normally finish within 90 days. Subsistence allowance is 50% of wages for the first 90 days and 75% after that, if the delay isn't the worker's fault.
