## Appointment letters for everyone

The OSH Code requires every employer to issue an appointment letter to every employee on appointment, in the prescribed form. Employees already working when the Code started (21 November 2025) without a letter were to receive one within three months, by about 20 February 2026. If you missed that date, issue them now.

The central rules prescribe the format; state rules may differ. A good letter covers the employee's name and designation, the nature of employment (permanent, fixed-term or other), the date of joining, wages and their components, working hours, the social security that applies and the notice terms. Use the [appointment letter](/toolkit/appointment-letter/) template.

## Fixed-term employment

The Industrial Relations Code defines fixed-term employment as engaging a worker on a written contract for a fixed period, on three conditions:

1. Hours, wages, allowances and other benefits are no less than those of a permanent worker doing the same or similar work.
2. The worker gets every statutory benefit a permanent worker gets, in proportion to the period served, even if it is shorter than the usual qualifying period.
3. The worker is eligible for gratuity after one year of service under the contract.

When a fixed-term contract expires or isn't renewed, that isn't retrenchment, and the Model Standing Orders require no notice. Ending the contract early for reasons other than discipline may count as retrenchment, with the notice and compensation that brings ([Termination, grievances and disputes](/handbooks/labour-law/termination-grievance-and-disputes/)).

## Contract labour

The contract labour part of the OSH Code applies to any establishment that employed 50 or more contract workers on any day in the previous 12 months, and to any contractor who employed 50 or more. It doesn't apply where the work is only intermittent or casual. Work done on more than 120 days in the previous 12 months, or seasonal work on more than 60 days a year, isn't treated as intermittent.

### Core activities

Contract labour is prohibited in an establishment's **core activities**: what it was set up to do, and anything essential to that. Support services are not core unless they are the establishment's business. These include sanitation, security, canteen and catering, loading and unloading, housekeeping and laundry, gardening, transport, courier services, civil and construction work including maintenance, and support services in hospitals, educational institutions, guest houses and clubs.

### What the principal employer must do

Under the central OSH rules:

- **Wages:** the contractor pays by the 7th day after the wage period ends. If the contractor doesn't pay in full, the principal employer must pay the contract workers the wages or the unpaid balance. Build a right to set this off against the contractor's bills into the contract.
- **Bonus:** if the contractor doesn't pay the minimum bonus, the principal employer must, after written notice from the workers or their union.
- **Amenities:** the principal employer provides basic facilities at its premises, such as toilets, washrooms, drinking water, a first-aid kit, canteen and crèche.
- **Grievances:** set up a grievance mechanism for contract workers; grievances not resolved in 30 days go to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator.
- **For the contractor:** regularly employed contract workers get an annual increment of at least 2%, and an experience certificate on request. A contractor can hold one licence covering several states or the whole country.

## Standing orders

Standing orders set out service conditions such as classes of workers, shifts, attendance, leave, misconduct, discipline, termination and grievances. Under the Industrial Relations Code they are required in industrial establishments with 300 or more workers on any day in the previous 12 months. The old central threshold was 100.

- The Model Standing Orders, 2026 (notified on 8 May 2026) have schedules for mines, manufacturing and services.
- An employer that adopts the model orders is treated as having certified standing orders, though the certifying officer can direct changes.
- Existing certified standing orders continue where they don't conflict with the Code.
- Under the model orders, probation is six months, extendable by three, and a permanent worker's employment is ended with one month's notice or pay.

Below 300 workers, standing orders aren't required, but written service rules or an employee handbook do the same job and prevent disputes.

## Inter-state migrant workers

The OSH Code's migrant worker provisions apply to establishments that employed 10 or more inter-state migrant workers on any day in the previous 12 months. An inter-state migrant worker is someone from another state, employed directly or through a contractor, earning up to ₹18,000 a month.

- **Journey allowance:** once a year, the fare for a return trip to the worker's native place. State rules set the qualifying service, often 180 days in 12 months.
- **Public distribution system:** the Code provides for migrant workers to keep access to ration benefits; state rules set out how.
- **Helpline:** the Code provides for a toll-free helpline for migrant workers.

The old displacement allowance under the 1979 Act has not been carried into the OSH Code.

## Remote work and employee data

The Codes don't set general rules for working from home. The one specific provision lets a woman work from home after maternity benefit ends, on agreed terms. Put remote work terms in the appointment letter and a policy. Wages, PF, ESI and leave rules apply wherever the employee works.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to employee data. Its main duties for employers, including notice, security safeguards and breach reporting, apply from about May 2027. Start mapping what employee data you hold and why.
