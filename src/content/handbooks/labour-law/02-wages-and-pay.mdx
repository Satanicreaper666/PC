## What counts as wages

The Code on Wages starts from "all remuneration", whether paid as salary, allowances or otherwise, and names basic pay, dearness allowance (DA) and retaining allowance as wages. It then lists what is excluded. The Code on Social Security uses the same definition, so it now drives gratuity, PF and ESI as well as overtime and bonus.

The exclusions are:

- (a) statutory bonus that isn't part of contractual pay
- (b) the value of housing, light, water, medical care or other amenities
- (c) the employer's PF or pension contribution
- (d) conveyance allowance or travel concessions
- (e) sums paid to cover special expenses of the job
- (f) house rent allowance (HRA)
- (g) payments under an award or settlement
- (h) overtime allowance
- (i) commission
- (j) gratuity on termination
- (k) retrenchment compensation, retirement benefits and ex gratia paid on termination

**The 50% test:** if items (a) to (i) together come to more than half of all remuneration, the amount above half is treated as wages. Gratuity, retrenchment compensation and retirement benefits sit outside the test. Pay in kind counts as wages up to 15% of total wages.

### What the add-back changes

- **Gratuity** is worked out on the higher wage figure, so the liability rises for employees whose excluded items were above half of pay.
- **PF** is a percentage of wages. Where PF is restricted to the wage ceiling (<Fact id="epf.wageCeiling" />), the add-back makes no difference for an employee whose wages were already above the ceiling. Where PF is paid on actual wages, or wages were below the ceiling, it rises.
- **ESI, overtime, bonus and leave encashment** all use the same definition, so recheck each.
- **Take-home pay** falls only where a deduction such as PF goes up. Explain any change to employees before it happens, and note that changing pay or benefits needs 21 days' notice to affected workers under the Industrial Relations Code.

## Minimum wages and the floor wage

Minimum wages now apply to every employee in every kind of employment, not just to listed "scheduled" jobs. Each appropriate government sets them mainly by skill (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled and highly skilled), and may vary them by area and by how arduous the work is, for example heat, humidity or hazardous work. Rates should be reviewed or revised at intervals of no more than five years.

The Centre fixes a national **floor wage**, which may differ by region, and no state minimum wage may be set below it. A floor wage amount had not been notified as of mid-2026; check the latest notification.

Under the central rules, minimum wages are fixed per day. The hourly rate is the daily rate divided by 8, and the monthly rate is the daily rate multiplied by 26.

## When wages must be paid

| Wage period | Pay by |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | The end of the shift |
| Weekly | The last working day before the weekly holiday |
| Fortnightly | Before the end of the second day after the fortnight |
| Monthly | Before the 7th day of the following month |

The old rule that let large employers pay by the 10th has gone. A wage period can't be longer than a month. Wages can be paid in cash, by cheque, by bank credit or electronically; a bank transfer leaves the clearest record.

### Paying people who leave

When an employee resigns, or is removed, dismissed or retrenched, or loses their job because the establishment closes, the wages due must be paid within two working days. This applies to every employee, including managers, because this part of the Code has no salary ceiling.

<Callout type="warning" title="The start date of the two days is not settled">
The Code says "within two working days of … resignation". Commentators read this as two working days after the resignation takes effect (the last working day), not after the letter is handed in. There was no official clarification as of 2 November 2026. Plan to pay within two working days of the last working day, and don't hold wages back while clearance forms are pending.

## Deductions

An employer may deduct only what the Code allows: fines, deductions for absence, damage or loss, housing and amenities provided, recovery of advances and loans, taxes and statutory levies, PF and similar contributions, and a few others. In any wage period, total deductions can't exceed 50% of the wages. Anything above that has to wait for later pay periods or be settled another way.

## Overtime

Overtime is paid at no less than twice the normal rate of wages, and under the OSH Code a worker can be asked to work overtime only with their consent. The central rules treat work beyond 8 hours a day for daily-wage staff, or beyond 48 hours a week for others, as overtime. [Leave and working hours](/handbooks/labour-law/leave-and-working-hours/) shows how to work out the rate and the quarterly cap.

## Equal pay

No employer may discriminate on the ground of gender, including against transgender persons, in wages or in recruitment for the same work or work of a similar nature. An employer can't fix an unequal position by cutting anyone's wages. For equal pay comparisons, conveyance, HRA, award payments and overtime are counted as wages.

## Records and claims

Keep a register of employees, a muster roll and a wage register, display the notice the Code requires (including wage rates, the wage period and pay day) and give every employee a wage slip. An employee can claim unpaid wages within three years. The penalties are in [Penalties](/handbooks/labour-law/penalties/).
