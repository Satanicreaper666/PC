## How enforcement works now

Each Code replaces the old "inspector" with an Inspector-cum-Facilitator, who advises as well as enforces, under an inspection scheme set by the government. For several Code on Wages offences (breaches other than underpayment, and record-keeping failures), the Inspector-cum-Facilitator must first give the employer a written direction with a time to comply. If the employer complies, there is no prosecution. That chance isn't given for a repeat of the same breach within five years.

## Penalties by law

### Code on Wages

| Offence | First offence | Repeat within five years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying less than is due | Fine up to ₹50,000 | Up to 3 months' imprisonment, a fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both |
| Any other breach, such as late payment or unauthorised deductions | Fine up to ₹20,000 | Up to 1 month's imprisonment, a fine up to ₹40,000, or both |
| Not keeping records properly | Fine up to ₹10,000 | No separate repeat penalty |

Employees can also claim unpaid amounts within three years.

### Code on Social Security

| Offence | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Not paying the employee's contribution deducted from wages | 1 to 3 years' imprisonment and a ₹1 lakh fine |
| Not paying other contributions | Imprisonment and a fine; check the terms in s.133 of the Social Security Code |
| Not paying gratuity that is due | Up to 1 year's imprisonment, a fine up to ₹50,000, or both |
| Penalising a woman in breach of the maternity or ESI provisions, not giving maternity benefit, obstructing an inspector, not producing registers, or a dishonestly false return | Up to 6 months' imprisonment, a fine up to ₹50,000, or both |
| Deducting the employer's share from wages, reducing wages or benefits unlawfully, not filing returns, other breaches | Fine up to ₹50,000 |

A court can impose a shorter prison term for adequate and special reasons recorded in the judgement. Late payments also carry simple interest of 12% a year, and EPFO and ESIC can't start proceedings for dues more than five years after they became due.

### Industrial Relations Code

As commonly summarised:

- **Lay-off, retrenchment or closure breaches in Chapter X establishments:** fines of ₹1 lakh to ₹10 lakh; for a repeat, ₹5 lakh to ₹20 lakh or up to six months' imprisonment, or both.
- **Not having standing orders where required:** fines of ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh, plus up to ₹2,000 a day while it continues.
- **Illegal strikes and lock-outs:** taking part in, encouraging or funding one can lead to fines and up to a month's imprisonment.

Check the exact amounts in the Code before relying on them for a specific case.

### OSH Code

- **General penalty:** where a breach of the Code, its rules or an order happens in an establishment, the employer or principal employer can be fined ₹2 lakh to ₹3 lakh, with up to ₹2,000 a day more if the breach continues after conviction.
- **A breach that causes death:** as commonly summarised, imprisonment for up to two years, a fine of up to ₹5 lakh, or both.

### POSH Act

Fine of up to ₹50,000 for not constituting an Internal Committee, not acting on its recommendations or otherwise breaking the Act. A repeat can bring up to twice the penalty and cancellation of a licence or registration.

## Compounding

Compounding lets an employer settle an eligible offence by paying a set sum, before or after prosecution starts, instead of going through trial.

Under the IR Code, compounded amounts go to the Social Security Fund, and an employer who doesn't comply with a compounding order pays an extra 20% of the maximum fine. ESIC issued a procedure for compounding under the Social Security Code in August 2026, which gives applicants 15 days from the notice.
