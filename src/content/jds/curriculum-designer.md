summary: "This role designs courses that help adults learn job-ready skills, from learning objectives to assessments and projects. Success means learners who master the skills, shown by their work and not just their satisfaction scores."
responsibilities:
  - "Design course structures, learning pathways and assessment frameworks for professional upskilling programmes"
  - "Write and review learning objectives using Bloom's Taxonomy aligned to industry competency frameworks"
  - "Develop storyboards, scripts and facilitator guides for video-based and live online instruction"
  - "Build knowledge checks, graded assignments, case studies and capstone project briefs"
  - "Review and QA educator-created content against the learning design brief and quality rubrics"
  - "Partner with industry experts and hiring managers to validate curriculum relevance to job-readiness outcomes"
  - "Analyse learner performance data: completion rates, quiz scores and assignment performance to iterate course design"

  - { name: "Instructional design (ADDIE and SAM)", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }

mustHave:
  - "A degree in education, instructional design, psychology or the subject being taught"
  - "Has designed courses or curricula, ideally for adult or professional learners"
  - "Uses an instructional design model such as ADDIE or SAM"
  - "A portfolio with learning objectives, assessments and content samples"
goodToHave:
  - "An instructional design certificate"
  - "Uses learner data to improve courses"
  - "Knows a subject area such as data, product, coding or finance"
interview:
  - q: "Here's a job description for a data analyst. Outline a four-week course that takes someone with no SQL to job-ready. Talk me through your approach."
    look: "Works back from the tasks in the JD, sequences skills with practice and feedback, assesses with realistic tasks and is honest about what four weeks can achieve. Keep this to a conversation or a short outline, not a full course."
  - q: "How do you judge whether a course produces learning, not just satisfied learners?"
    look: "Uses assessments aligned to the objectives, looks at performance on realistic tasks and, where possible, at results on the job, alongside feedback."
  - q: "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a subject expert's content because it didn't serve the learning objective."
    look: "Explains the learner's need, uses the objective as the shared reference, respects the expert's knowledge and reaches agreement."
greenFlags:
  - "Portfolio shows clear objectives with assessments that match them"
  - "Has redesigned a course using learner data and shown the improvement"
  - "Worked through a difficult collaboration with a subject expert to a good result"
redFlags:
  - "Treats content production as course design: attractive material with no learning structure"
  - "Can't explain how learning objectives shape assessment"
  - "Doesn't distinguish between engaging learners and helping them use skills at work"
