level: Manager

stage: recruit
checked: 2026-11-02
summary: "This role is the HR partner for two or three technology business units, advising leaders on organisation design, performance, retention and employee relations. Success means people decisions made on evidence, fewer surprises in attrition and employee relations cases closed fairly."
responsibilities:
  - "Act as the main HR partner for 2–3 business units of about 400–800 people, and contribute in their leadership meetings"
  - "Lead headcount planning and organisation design with finance and business leaders"
  - "Design and run engagement, retention and career-path programmes for technology staff"
  - "Handle employee relations cases end to end: fair investigation, documentation, resolution and closure"
  - "Run performance management: calibration, feedback and improvement plans that are fair and well documented"
  - "Work with talent acquisition on key hires, role profiles and structured debriefs"
  - "Analyse attrition, engagement and pay data and present it to leaders every month"
  - "Keep people practices in line with the four Labour Codes (in force from 21 November 2025), the POSH Act and company policy"
competencies:
  - { name: "Organisation design and workforce planning", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Employee relations and labour law", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "People analytics", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Change management", type: Behavioural, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Stakeholder influence", type: Behavioural, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "Business acumen", type: Behavioural, level: "Proficient" }
mustHave:
  - "Has worked as an HRBP or HR generalist for technology teams, typically over five or more years"
  - "Knows Indian employment law in practice: the four Labour Codes, the POSH Act, and PF and ESI"
  - "Has supported 300 or more employees on their own"
  - "Has influenced senior leaders on a people decision and can describe the result"
goodToHave:
  - "Experience in a fast-growing company"
  - "Has partnered engineering or product teams"
  - "An HR certification such as SHRM-CP"
interview:
  - q: "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a business leader about a people decision. What was at stake?"
    look: "Explains the risk (legal, fairness or retention), uses data, offers an alternative and shows how the working relationship held up. Notice whether they protect confidential details while telling the story."
  - q: "Attrition in engineering has suddenly risen. Walk me through your first 30 days."
    look: "Checks the data first (who, where, regretted or not), talks to leavers and stayers, separates causes such as pay, managers and the work itself, and agrees a few actions with owners."
  - q: "Describe how you run a performance improvement plan, from diagnosis to closure."
    look: "Starts with clear, documented expectations and support, sets a fair timeline with regular reviews, and treats the plan as a genuine chance to improve rather than a route to exit."
greenFlags:
  - "Has led a restructuring or reorganisation and handled the communication"
  - "Can explain their business unit's strategy, not just its HR plan"
  - "Takes part in business reviews as a full participant"
redFlags:
  - "Describes the HRBP role as helping with hiring and engagement events"
  - "Has never handled a complex employee relations case on their own"
  - "Speaks only in HR terms and can't connect them to business results"
