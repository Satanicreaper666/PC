summary: "This early-career role builds the models, research and slides that consulting teams use to make recommendations. Success means accurate analysis, findings that say why they matter, and work that needs little correction before it reaches the client."
responsibilities:
  - "Build financial models: 3-statement, scenario analysis, DCF valuations and market sizing models"
  - "Conduct secondary research: industry reports, competitor analysis, regulatory filings and academic literature"
  - "Prepare PowerPoint deliverables: structure, formatting and data visualisation to partner-ready standard"
  - "Support primary research: transcribe expert interviews, synthesise call notes and extract themes"
  - "Analyse large datasets using Excel, SQL or Python; surface insights relevant to the client hypothesis"
  - "Track project workplan; flag dependencies and deadline risks to the engagement manager"
  - "Contribute to proposal development: slide structuring, market sizing and capability narratives"

mustHave:
  - "A degree in economics, engineering, statistics, business or a related subject"
  - "Builds financial models in Excel; you'll be asked to show this in the interview"
  - "Checks work carefully and catches errors in numbers"
  - "Can turn a long piece of research into a short, clear summary"
goodToHave:
  - "An internship at a consulting firm, bank or investment fund"
  - "Python or R for data analysis"
  - "Progress towards a finance qualification such as CFA Level 1"
interview:
  - q: "A retail chain wants to double its number of stores in three years. What three questions would you want answered before building a financial model?"
    look: "Asks about the economics of existing stores, the supply of good locations, and the funding and management capacity to expand, and explains why each matters."
  - q: "Walk me through a financial model you built. What were the key assumptions and sensitivities?"
    look: "Explains the structure clearly, knows which assumptions drive the result, has tested them and can say what would change the conclusion."
  - q: "How would you turn ten pages of research into three slides without losing what matters?"
    look: "Starts from the question the reader needs answered, leads with the conclusion, keeps the key evidence and moves detail to an appendix."
greenFlags:
  - "Can present a model or research paper they built independently"
  - "Has examples of data analysis from study, an internship or a project"
  - "Turns an unfamiliar problem into a clear plan of analysis"
redFlags:
  - "Can't build a basic three-statement model in the time given"
  - "Research describes what happened but not why it matters"
  - "Can't explain the logic of a formula in their own model"
