summary: "This role explains the company's medicines to doctors and pharmacies in a territory, using approved clinical information. Success means steady growth in prescriptions, trusted relationships with doctors and full compliance with the rules on samples, gifts and promotion."
responsibilities:
  - "Promote and detail assigned pharmaceutical products to target physicians, specialists and hospital pharmacies within the assigned territory"
  - "Achieve monthly prescription and sales targets; track chemist-level offtake using mobile CRM tools"
  - "Build relationships with 80–120 doctors, follow up regularly and take part in continuing medical education (CME) events"
  - "Execute brand campaigns, product launches and sample distribution in line with marketing guidelines"
  - "Gather competitive intelligence: competitor detailing activity, pricing and new product launches"
  - "Participate in weekly territory review meetings; submit daily call reports via CRM app"
  - "Follow the pharmaceutical industry's marketing code and the company's policies on samples, gifts and meetings with doctors"

mustHave:
  - "A degree or diploma in pharmacy or life sciences (B.Pharm, D.Pharm or B.Sc.)"
  - "Explains clinical information clearly and accurately"
  - "Can travel within the assigned territory every working day"
  - "A valid driving licence, if the territory needs you to drive"
goodToHave:
  - "A diploma in pharmaceutical management"
  - "An internship or earlier job as a medical representative"
  - "Experience with CRM or digital sales tools"
interview:
  - q: "How would you plan your first month in a territory where prescriptions have been falling?"
    look: "Studies the data first (prescribers, chemists and competitors), ranks doctors by potential, plans a visit cycle and sets a few simple measures to track."
  - q: "Show me how you would explain a product to a sceptical cardiologist in three minutes."
    look: "Leads with the evidence that matters for the doctor's patients, is accurate about benefits and side effects, listens to objections and never overstates a claim."
  - q: "How would you handle a doctor who refuses to meet medical representatives?"
    look: "Respects the decision, looks for permitted alternatives such as written information or CME events, and never offers inducements."

  - "Can't name the main competing products for the medicine they would promote"
