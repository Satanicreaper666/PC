level: Senior
experience: "4–7 years"
stage: recruit
checked: 2026-11-02
summary: "This role leads design for one or two product areas, from research to shipped screens. Success means design decisions grounded in user evidence, a consistent design system and products that everyone can use, including people with disabilities."
responsibilities:
  - "Lead design for 1–2 product areas: discovery, wireframes, prototypes and delivery"
  - "Run user research, including interviews, usability tests and observing people at work"
  - "Build and maintain the design system and reusable components in Figma"
  - "Work daily with product managers and engineers as a full member of the team"
  - "Run design critiques, present to leaders and make the case for decisions centred on users"
  - "Set accessibility standards (WCAG 2.2 level AA) and check that designs are built to them"
  - "Use evidence such as funnel data and session recordings to inform design decisions"
competencies:
  - { name: "Product and interaction design", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "User research methods", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "Figma and prototyping", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "Design systems", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Accessibility (WCAG)", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Facilitation and storytelling", type: Behavioural, level: "Proficient" }
mustHave:
  - "Has a portfolio of shipped product work that shows the problem, the process and the outcome"
  - "Can show design decisions driven by research, not only by visual taste"
  - "Works fluently in Figma, including components and auto layout"
  - "Explains design reasoning clearly to engineers and executives"
goodToHave:
  - "Has designed for low-bandwidth, regional-language or first-time internet users"
  - "Motion design skills"
  - "Has designed complex B2B workflows"
interview:
  - q: "Walk me through a project where research changed the direction of the design."
    look: "Describes what they learned, how it changed the problem they were solving and the evidence that the new direction worked."
  - q: "Tell me about a design you were proud of that didn't work for users. What did you learn?"
    look: "Owns the result, explains how they found out and what they changed, both in the design and in their own process."
  - q: "How do you keep design quality and consistency when engineering is moving fast?"
    look: "Relies on a design system, early involvement in planning, clear specifications and light reviews, and makes sensible trade-offs rather than insisting on perfection."
greenFlags:
  - "Portfolio shows the problem, the process and the measured outcome, not just finished screens"
  - "Can describe a decision they reversed after user research"
  - "Has turned a proposed solution into a better-defined problem with the product manager"
redFlags:
  - "Portfolio shows final screens only, with no research or iteration"
  - "Can't explain the difference between exploratory and evaluative research"
  - "Becomes defensive when design decisions are questioned"
