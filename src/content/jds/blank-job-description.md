## How to fill in each part

A job description is your first filter. Write the parts in this order: each answers a question a good candidate is asking. The [chapter on writing job descriptions](/handbooks/recruitment/writing-job-descriptions/) explains each part with examples.

1. **Why the role exists.** Two or three lines on what the person will achieve in their first six months. Leave out adjectives such as "dynamic" or "self-motivated".
2. **What they'll do.** Five to seven lines, each a real deliverable that starts with a verb: build, run, own, fix, ship.
3. **What they need.** Three to five things they need in the first months. Name skills and results ("has run payroll for 300 people") rather than years or degrees, unless the work truly needs them.
4. **Good to have.** Two or three extras, clearly labelled, so people who meet the must-haves still apply.
5. **Pay, place and terms.** The pay range, location and work mode, employment type and who the role reports to.
6. **How to apply.** One clear step, what happens next and when you'll reply.

Then build the interview kit: pick four or five competencies from what the person needs, write one question for each and agree what a strong answer shows before the first interview.
