summary: "This role is the company's in-house lawyer, advising teams on contracts, company law, employment law and regulation. Success means sound contracts closed on time, risks explained in business terms and no avoidable regulatory surprises."
responsibilities:
  - "Draft, review and negotiate commercial contracts: MSAs, vendor agreements, SaaS subscription agreements, NDAs, employment contracts and IP assignment deeds"
  - "Advise on company law matters: board resolutions, shareholder agreements, ESOP scheme documentation and Companies Act compliance"
  - "Advise HR on employment law: the four Labour Codes (in force from 21 November 2025), the POSH Act and state shops and establishments laws"
  - "Manage regulatory compliance calendar: ROC filings, FEMA/FDI compliance, GST/direct tax coordination with finance"
  - "Support fundraising and M&A transactions: data room management, due diligence coordination and legal condition precedent closure"
  - "Monitor legal and regulatory developments relevant to the company's business; issue internal advisories proactively"
  - "Manage external law firm relationships: briefing, cost control and quality review of external counsel output"
  - "Advise on data protection under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, whose main duties apply from May 2027"

mustHave:
  - "A law degree (LLB) and several years of practice"
  - "At least two years in-house, or law-firm experience advising businesses directly"
  - "Drafts commercial contracts, such as a master services agreement, without relying on a template"
  - "Knows the Companies Act, 2013, FEMA and Indian employment law, including the four Labour Codes"
goodToHave:
  - "An LLM in corporate or commercial law"
  - "Experience in a regulated technology, fintech or healthcare company"
  - "Experience with cross-border transactions or GIFT City"
interview:
  - q: "How would you negotiate a data processing agreement with a critical software vendor who refuses our standard terms?"
    look: "Separates must-haves (security, breach notice, sub-processors and deletion) from nice-to-haves, understands the vendor's limits, offers alternatives and records any remaining risk for a business decision. Knows that most DPDP duties apply from May 2027, and that contracts signed now should anticipate them."
  - q: "A business team wants to onboard a vendor in 24 hours, and you have concerns about the contract. What do you do?"
    look: "Focuses on the few terms that really matter, offers a fast, safe route such as a short-form agreement or interim safeguards, and makes clear who owns the decision and the risk."
  - q: "What are the main legal points for a company issuing ESOPs to employees in India?"
    look: "Covers the scheme and its approvals under the Companies Act, eligibility, vesting and exercise terms, tax when options are exercised and when shares are sold, foreign exchange rules where a foreign parent is involved, and clear communication to employees."
greenFlags:
  - "Explains a legal risk in terms of its business consequence"
  - "Has unblocked a stalled negotiation without escalating to outside counsel"
  - "Tracks regulatory changes and briefs the business before being asked"
redFlags:
  - "Treats every change to standard terms as a deal-breaker, with no sense of proportion"
  - "Has handled legal matters but never worked closely with business teams"
  - "Can't draft a basic NDA or employment contract without a precedent"
