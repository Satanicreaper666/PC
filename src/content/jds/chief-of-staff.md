summary: "This role works alongside the founder or CEO to turn priorities into action across the company. Success means leadership meetings that end in decisions, cross-team projects that finish on time and a CEO whose time goes to the most important work."
responsibilities:
  - "Work with the CEO on board papers, investor updates and company-wide communication"
  - "Run the company's planning rhythm: quarterly goals, weekly reviews and monthly cross-team check-ins"
  - "Lead or support priority projects such as fundraising, due diligence or entering a new market"
  - "Coordinate the CEO's external commitments, such as talks, press briefings and industry forums"
  - "Spot and fix problems that fall between teams"
  - "Make sure big decisions involve the right people and information at the right time"
  - "Set up the leadership team's routines: meetings, decision records and follow-through"

mustHave:
  - "Has worked in strategy, consulting, investment banking or an early startup team"
  - "Has led cross-team work to completion without formal authority"
  - "Writes clearly, from board memos to investor updates"
  - "Works well when the scope changes from week to week"
goodToHave:
  - "A previous chief of staff or strategy role at a startup"
  - "Experience with fundraising, M&A or corporate development"
  - "Knows the regulation that affects the company's sector"
interview:
  - q: "Describe a time you had to get two senior leaders with very different views to agree."
    look: "Understands each side's interests, brings facts and options, sets up a decision and keeps both leaders' trust. The credit goes to the process, not to winning."
  - q: "The founder asks you to do five things at once and you can do two well. What do you do?"
    look: "Clarifies the goal behind each request, proposes priorities with reasons, agrees them openly and hands off or drops the rest explicitly."
  - q: "Walk me through a document you wrote that changed a major decision."
    look: "Explains the question, the structure of the argument, the evidence and what the reader decided. If they share the document, it should be clear and concise."

  - "Asks what success looks like in six months before asking about scope"
redFlags:
  - "Sees the role as strategy only and shows no interest in operational follow-through"
  - "Can't explain how they influenced a decision they didn't own"
