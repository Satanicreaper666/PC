level: Manager

checked: 2026-11-02
summary: "This role leads a team of six to ten software engineers and is measured by what the team delivers and how its people grow. Most of the time goes on people, planning and engineering standards rather than on writing code."
responsibilities:
  - "Lead a team of 6–10 engineers: hiring, growth plans and performance reviews"
  - "Plan sprints and scope technical work with product and design"
  - "Set and keep engineering standards for code review, test coverage and incident response"
  - "Clear blockers early and raise risks before they turn into incidents"
  - "Set quarterly goals for the team and track delivery against them"
  - "Build a team where people raise problems openly and give each other regular feedback"
  - "Report on team health and delivery measures such as cycle time, deployment frequency and time to recover from incidents"
competencies:
  - { name: "People management and coaching", type: Behavioural, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "System design", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Agile delivery", type: Technical, level: "Expert" }
  - { name: "Engineering metrics (DORA)", type: Technical, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Resolving conflict", type: Behavioural, level: "Proficient" }
  - { name: "Hiring and assessment", type: Behavioural, level: "Proficient" }
mustHave:
  - "Several years as a software engineer and at least two years managing engineers directly"
  - "Can review code and system design credibly, especially for distributed systems"
  - "Has helped engineers grow through regular career conversations, including at least one promotion they supported"
  - "Can explain an engineering plan clearly to people who aren't engineers"
goodToHave:
  - "Has grown a team from about 5 to 15 or more people"
  - "Experience with platform or infrastructure engineering"
  - "Has introduced a practice, such as blameless post-mortems, that the team kept using"
interview:
  - q: "Tell me about an engineer on your team who was struggling. How did you work out what was wrong, and what happened?"
    look: "Separates skill, motivation and circumstances, describes regular, specific conversations and a clear plan, and is honest about the outcome, including when the person moved on."
  - q: "How do you protect engineering quality when the business is pushing for speed?"
    look: "Gives a real example of negotiating scope or timelines, explains the risk in business terms and shows how agreed standards such as tests and reviews held."
  - q: "Describe how you handled a sharp technical disagreement between two senior engineers."
    look: "Brings the disagreement into the open, sets a fair way to decide, such as data, a prototype or a time-boxed review, and keeps both people engaged once the decision is made."
greenFlags:
  - "Has helped at least one engineer reach a promotion and can explain how"
  - "Shows data for a process change that cut cycle time or incidents"
  - "References describe specific support they gave the people who reported to them"
redFlags:
  - "Describes success mostly through their own code rather than the team's results"
  - "Can't name the growth goals of the people they manage"
  - "Sees management as running stand-ups and updating tickets"
